## Answer

Design agencies use hourly rates, flat fees, retainers, value-based pricing, performance-based pricing, and subscription services. Subscription services typically cost $699–$2,599/month for unlimited design. Retainers usually range from $1,000 to $15,000+/month for comprehensive design services.

## Summary
Design agencies in 2026 commonly use productized subscription models (often marketed as “unlimited design”) alongside retainers, hourly, and traditional agency engagements. Published ranges show subscriptions from roughly $200 to $2,995 per month, freelancers charging $25–$150 per hour, and agencies quoting from $5,000 to $100,000+ for engagements; specific offers vary by provider and what’s included [3][1][8][7].

## Key Findings
1) How “unlimited” design subscriptions work: flat monthly fee with unlimited requests and revisions; some providers allow pausing/canceling anytime and encourage submitting as many design tasks as needed [1][2][8].  
2) Price ranges: subscription services are cited at $200–$2,995 per month; freelancers at $25–$150 per hour; and agencies at $5,000–$100,000+ per engagement. One concrete example: Penji starts at $499/month for unlimited requests with fast turnarounds and 120+ design categories [3][8].  
3) Retainers vs subscriptions: sources distinguish agency retainers from subscriptions and frame the choice between them; the retainer discussion centers on monthly fees, while subscriptions emphasize unlimited requests for a flat monthly price [1].  
4) “Designjoy-style” model: Designjoy promotes a subscription that lets clients request “as many designs as you’d like,” with the ability to pause or cancel at any time, illustrating the mechanics and positioning of this category [2].  
5) Productized agency pricing for scale: analyses of agency pricing models emphasize approaches that support predictable recurring revenue—directly relevant to subscriptions and retainers for productized agencies [6].  
6) Market mapping and comparisons: there are roundups that detail “unlimited design” providers’ plan costs and inclusions, and SaaS-focused lists that stress differences in delivery and process after a subscription starts—not just sticker price [7][5].  
7) Buyer perspective: small-business community discussions show active exploration of which subscription pricing structures best fit SMB budgets and needs, indicating demand-side interest in clarity and affordability [4].  
8) Speed and transparency as selling points: subscription providers highlight clear monthly costs and rapid talent matching (e.g., within 24 hours) as key advantages over other models [3].

  - Mechanics: Subscriptions are marketed as a one-stop, flat monthly price with unlimited requests and revisions [1]. Penji likewise advertises “unlimited design requests,” fast turnaround, and broad category coverage starting at $499/month [8].  
  - Pricing benchmarks: A broad 2026 view places subscription costs between $200 and $2,995 per month, positioning them as more predictable than ad-hoc engagements [3]. Individual provider pages and roundups supply specific plan details; Digital Polo compiles what each “unlimited” provider includes and charges, while Penji publishes a concrete entry point at $499/month [7][8].  
  - Differentiation beyond price: Analyses aimed at SaaS teams argue that the real differentiation emerges after signup (process, quality, handoff, outcomes), not just in the nominal pricing model, suggesting buyers should scrutinize delivery model and workflow expectations [5].

- Agency retainers vs subscriptions:  
  - Subscriptions emphasize unlimited requests/revisions and simplicity at a flat fee [1].  
  - Retainers are framed as a separate, ongoing monthly arrangement; the ManyPixels piece positions the decision as a tradeoff between the structures and references average monthly costs for retainers, though specific figures are not provided in the excerpt [1].  
  - In the context of productized agencies, recurring arrangements (subscriptions/retainers) are highlighted for helping scale with predictable revenue [6].

- Hourly, agency project fees, and comparative costs:  
  - Freelancers: $25–$150 per hour—a wide span that reflects experience/scope differences [3].  
  - Agencies: $5,000–$100,000+ for engagements, signaling significant variance by scope and specialization [3].  
  - Subscriptions: $200–$2,995/month range, with examples like $499/month for Penji indicating where “unlimited” services may sit for SMBs and startups [3][8].  
  - These comparisons underline the subscription model’s appeal for budget predictability and speed-to-start (e.g., talent matching within 24 hours), versus the flexibility of hourly and the breadth/depth of traditional agency engagements [3].

- “Designjoy-style” subscription positioning:  
  - Designjoy prominently markets a single-subscription setup—“one subscription to rule them all”—paired with unlimited requests and pause/cancel flexibility, encapsulating the category’s promise of simplicity and control for buyers who prefer continuous, queue-based design throughput [2].  
  - This aligns with broader market content that catalogs unlimited providers and their inclusions, suggesting buyers should examine what “unlimited” covers in practice (turnaround, active task limits, categories) as outlined by pricing breakdown roundups [7].

- Productized agency pricing strategy and scale:  
  - Productized agencies are encouraged to adopt pricing models that scale with predictable recurring revenue—subscriptions and retainers are natural fits—according to guidance comparing agency pricing models in 2026 [6].  
  - For SaaS and growth teams, lists of subscription agencies emphasize results and delivery discipline after onboarding; the flat fee matters, but outcomes and process ultimately drive value realization [5].

- Buyer signals and suitability for SMBs:  
  - Community discussions (e.g., small business forums) probe which subscription structures best align with smaller budgets and needs, reflecting an active search for clarity and affordability in design services [4].  
  - Roundups of “every major unlimited graphic design service” imply accessible, comparable options for SMBs and startups seeking to benchmark providers on both price and scope [7].
