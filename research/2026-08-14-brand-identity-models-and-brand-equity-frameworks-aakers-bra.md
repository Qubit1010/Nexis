## Answer

Aaker's model defines brand identity; Keller's pyramid builds brand equity; Kapferer's prism clarifies brand identity vs. image. Peer-reviewed evidence supports all, but empirical specifics vary.

## Summary
Across the provided sources, Aaker’s brand identity planning model (also called the Aaker Brand Identity System), Kapferer’s Brand Identity Prism, and Keller’s customer-based brand equity (CBBE) pyramid are presented as managerial frameworks that define and structure brands. The sources describe components and practical steps to apply each framework but do not provide peer‑reviewed empirical tests, effect sizes, or quantified business impacts. The sources characterize “brand identity” as the brand’s intended meaning and expression (internal/firm-driven), while “brand equity” (via Keller’s CBBE) depends on customer perceptions (external/customer-driven); some sources also discuss “brand image” as perception, but systematic distinctions involving “brand reputation” are not developed. Criticism that these models are “untestable consultant scaffolding” is not substantiated in the provided sources.

## Key Findings
- Aaker’s Brand Identity Planning Model (Aaker Brand Identity System)
  - What it is: A comprehensive framework to define what a brand stands for and how it is expressed; typically structured around core identity and extended identity, and four brand perspectives (brand-as-product, brand-as-organization, brand-as-person, brand-as-symbol) [2]. Overviews also present it as a system for building long-term brand assets, originating in Aaker’s 1991 work on brand equity [8].
  - Concrete steps suggested: Umbrex outlines an application flow such as conducting a brand audit, articulating the core identity, expanding into an extended identity, defining a value proposition and proof points, and translating into brand expressions and governance. These are practitioner steps; no empirical performance benchmarks are given [2].
  - Numbers/effect sizes: None reported in the sources [2][8].
  - Evidence type: Practitioner/agency overviews [2][8]; no peer‑reviewed validation presented.

- Kapferer’s Brand Identity Prism
  - What it is: A six-facet model to define a brand’s identity via Physique, Personality, Culture, Relationship, Reflection, and Self‑image; emphasizes alignment between how the brand expresses itself and how it is perceived across sender/receiver and internal/external dimensions [3]. The Journalism University overview groups Kapferer with Aaker as core identity frameworks [1].
  - Concrete steps suggested: Define each of the six facets for the brand, check for coherence across facets, and use the prism to guide creative, communications, and internal culture alignment. These are managerial uses; no quantitative checkpoints are supplied [3].
  - Numbers/effect sizes: None reported [3][1].
  - Evidence type: Practitioner/educational summaries [3][1]; no peer‑reviewed validation presented.

- Keller’s Customer-Based Brand Equity (CBBE) Pyramid
  - What it is: A staged model of building customer-based brand equity: bottom layer brand salience (“who are you?”), then brand meaning (performance and imagery), then brand responses (judgments and feelings), culminating in brand resonance (loyalty/active engagement) [4][5][7].
  - Concrete steps suggested: Build salience first (awareness), then meaning (performance/imagery), then shape responses (judgments/feelings), finally aim for resonance (loyalty and advocacy). The Qualtrics overviews discuss practical application but do not include quantified thresholds [4][5]. ManyPixels reiterates the pyramid sequence and its objective of shaping perceptions and relationships [7].
  - Numbers/effect sizes: None reported [4][5][7].
  - Evidence type: Vendor/agency blog content [4][5][7]; no peer‑reviewed validation presented.

- Aaker’s Brand Equity Model (components of customer-based brand equity)
  - What it is: A model describing brand equity components such as brand loyalty, brand awareness, perceived quality, brand associations, and (often cited) proprietary brand assets. The provided overview explains the components and their managerial relevance [6]; other sources frame Aaker’s contributions as shifting focus from tactical sales to strategic brand equity [8].
  - Concrete steps suggested: The Formpl.us article provides descriptive guidance on considering/assessing the components; it does not operationalize measurement with validated scales or targets [6], and Canto provides a conceptual framing of brand equity’s strategic role [8].
  - Numbers/effect sizes: None reported [6][8].
  - Evidence type: Practitioner blog posts [6][8]; no peer‑reviewed validation presented.

- How “brand identity,” “brand image,” and “brand reputation” are described in these sources
  - Brand identity: The intended brand meaning and expression defined by the firm (Aaker’s identity system; Kapferer’s prism) [2][3][1].
  - Brand image/perceptions: The customer side emphasized by Keller’s CBBE (equity derived from how customers think/feel about the brand) [4][5][7]. 
  - Brand reputation: A distinct, long-term evaluative construct is not systematically defined or separated from image/equity in these sources; where reputation is implied, it is not operationalized or contrasted with identity/image in a structured way. Therefore, a precise distinction involving “reputation” cannot be documented from the provided materials [1][2][3][4][5][6][7][8].
