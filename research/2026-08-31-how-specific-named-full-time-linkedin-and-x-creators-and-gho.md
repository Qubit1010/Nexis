## Detail
Justin Welsh (LinkedIn-first; also long‑form)  
- Deciding the single idea: Analyses of Welsh emphasize choosing a clear angle and then selecting from a small rotation of proven post formats to present that angle—he “grew… by rotating through… different kinds of posts,” which keeps execution focused while preventing sameness in the feed [8]. The Autoposting.ai teardown catalogs his “real content patterns, hook formulas, [and] posting system,” reinforcing that the single idea is embedded in a format choice with an appropriate hook, not an improvised riff [1].  
- Opening line beyond truncation: The same analysis highlights hook formulas (not one‑off lines) as part of a system—i.e., the opener’s job is to align with the format and promised outcome, not merely to pass “see more” [1]. Welsh’s own workshop stresses designing an experience, implying the hook sets the narrative or problem‑solving arc the post will deliver, not just a curiosity spike [4].  
- Pacing/closing multi‑post threads: None of the Welsh sources here document thread pacing/closing; the coverage focuses on single‑post formats and hooks [1][4][8].  
- Worked example (named; that succeeded): Pawan Singh’s teardown of a specific Welsh LinkedIn post with “11K‑like” performance extracts the “writing framework that made it stand out,” offering a concrete pattern you can mirror (e.g., how the hook, body, and payoff were arranged) [5].  
- Step‑by‑step technique you can adapt (as documented):  
  1) Select one format from Welsh’s rotation to match your idea (e.g., a pattern enumerated in the Autoposting.ai analysis) [1][8].  
  2) Choose a matching hook formula from those cataloged—so the opener frames the exact promise the format will fulfill [1].  
  3) Build the body to deliver the hook’s promise in that format (the systemized “posting system” is designed to make this repeatable) [1].  
  4) Vary formats across posts so each “looks different” while staying on your core themes [8].  
  5) For deeper structure and narrative cohesion, borrow principles from Welsh’s workshop about crafting an experience, not a mere explanation [4].

Dan Koe (X/LinkedIn; minimalist systems)  
- Deciding the single idea: A named teardown characterizes Koe’s output as “clean. Sharp. Almost blunt,” specifically noting the absence of long threads and heavy storytelling, which implies a discipline of isolating one high‑agency idea per post rather than multi‑concept sequences [2].  
- Opening line beyond truncation: In this minimalist frame, the line functions as a surgical thesis that positions the reader for decisive action or recognition—not as a teaser for a lengthy thread [2].  
- Pacing/closing multi‑post threads: The analysis explicitly contrasts Koe with “the usual creator playbook: long threads,” indicating he rarely relies on multi‑post pacing/closing at all—his system privileges concise, standalone units [2].  
- System/workflow for scale and validation: Koe “treat[s] content creation as a repeatable, architectural system,” using LLMs to deconstruct why viral posts work, and “using Twitter as a low‑cost litmus test to validate ideas before expanding them into newsletters and videos” [7].  
- Step‑by‑step technique you can adapt (as documented):  
  1) Use an LLM to deconstruct several high‑performing posts in your niche (Koe’s playbook treats this as a repeatable diagnostic) [7].  
  2) Synthesize one high‑agency idea into a blunt, minimalist post (avoid bloat and narrative sprawl) [2][7].  
  3) Ship it on X as a low‑cost test; only expand validated ideas into longer formats (newsletters/videos) [7].  
  4) Keep avoiding multi‑tweet threads unless necessary; the style benchmark is standalone clarity [2].
