## Answer

The sources indicate that Emirati patients in the UAE have a strong trust in traditional medicine and complementary and alternative medicine (CAM), with a high prevalence of CAM use among UAE nationals. The NYU Abu Dhabi Healthy Future Study aims to understand the high rates of obesity, diabetes, and heart disease among Emiratis, focusing on lifestyle, environment, and genetic risk factors. The study also highlights the importance of family decision-making and the influence of family history on health behaviors. While the sources do not provide a definitive answer on the exact level of trust Emirati patients have in doctors, they do suggest that doctors are seen as role models and that family history significantly influences health behaviors.

## Summary
Across the available UAE-based evidence, Emirati patients’ health beliefs and practices—including complementary and alternative medicine (CAM)/herbal use, cultural and family influences, and dietary practices—intersect with diabetes care, and more broadly with chronic disease management amid high burdens of diabetes and obesity. Qualitative work with UAE nationals highlights cultural and family contexts shaping type 2 diabetes management, while observational studies document substantial interest in herbal/CAM approaches. System-level reviews and cohort initiatives provide context for the magnitude of cardiometabolic disease and the need to align quality management with culturally responsive care. However, direct evidence in this set on trust in doctors and family decision-making dynamics as quantifiable drivers of diabetes/obesity outcomes is limited and remains an important gap.

## Key Findings
- Cultural context and family/lifestyle factors shape diabetes self-management among UAE nationals
  - A qualitative dissertation specifically examined how culture and lifestyle influence type 2 diabetes mellitus management among UAE nationals, underscoring the salience of cultural competency in care and patient education within familial and social contexts [1].

- CAM use is a recognized part of how some Emirati patients with type 2 diabetes manage their condition
  - A peer-reviewed UAE study investigated CAM use among patients with type 2 diabetes, characterizing commonly used modalities (including herbal remedies) and patients’ beliefs and practices around CAM as part of diabetes self-management [3][6].
  - The study highlights the need for clinicians to proactively elicit CAM use and provide guidance on safety and evidence, given its relevance to diabetes care in the UAE context [3][6].

- Herbal medicine use is widespread in the UAE general population and shaped by attitudes and access patterns
  - A national cross-sectional study of adults across seven emirates (n=448) documented patterns and attitudes toward herbal medicine use, indicating broad uptake and offering insight into sources of information and perceptions of benefits/risks within the UAE population [7].
  - Findings from this general-population lens complement diabetes-specific CAM findings by situating herbal use within wider Emirati health beliefs and day-to-day decision-making [7].

- Dietary practices among UAE patients with diabetes show room for improvement and require targeted support
  - An observational assessment of dietary practice among diabetic patients in the UAE identified gaps between recommended diabetes nutrition guidance and reported practices, reinforcing the importance of culturally adapted dietary counseling and support in routine care [5].

- System-level context: mapping quality management practices for diabetes, CVD, and obesity care in the UAE
  - A scoping review synthesized health quality management practices relevant to diabetes, cardiovascular disease, and obesity care in the UAE, providing a landscape view of existing approaches and areas for improvement that can support more culturally attuned, patient-centered services [2].

- High burdens of diabetes and obesity among Emiratis motivate longitudinal research
  - The Abu Dhabi Cohort Study is following Emirati adults to understand long-term health and lifestyle trends, motivated in part by the high rate of obesity, diabetes, and heart disease in the Emirati population; this initiative may generate future insights into how beliefs and behaviors evolve and affect outcomes over time [4].

## Evidence Quality
- Study designs
  - Qualitative: An in-depth dissertation explored cultural and lifestyle dimensions of diabetes management among UAE nationals, offering rich context but not quantifying effects [1].
  - Cross-sectional observational: CAM among UAE patients with type 2 diabetes [3][6], national herbal medicine use patterns [7], and dietary practices among UAE diabetic patients [5] provide snapshots of behaviors and beliefs but cannot establish causality or temporal change.
  - Scoping review: Maps practices for chronic disease care quality in the UAE but is not designed to quantify effectiveness of specific interventions [2].
  - News/initiative: The Abu Dhabi Cohort Study summary offers context but not peer-reviewed outcomes yet [4].

- Generalizability and bias
  - Convenience and online sampling (e.g., [7]) may limit representativeness; clinic-based samples (e.g., [3][5]) may not capture non–care-seeking individuals.
  - Temporal considerations: Dietary practice data are older (2008) [5]; CAM and herbal studies are more recent (2020–2022) [3][7]. System-level review is very recent [2].
  - Across this set, direct, quantified measures of trust in doctors or explicit metrics of family decision-making influence among Emirati patients are limited or absent. Where such constructs are discussed (e.g., cultural/family influences in qualitative work), findings are contextual rather than numerical [1]. The CAM literature included here addresses beliefs and behaviors but does not quantify trust in physicians per se [3][6][7].

- Consensus vs. contested
  - The evidence consistently indicates that cultural context and non-biomedical modalities (herbal/CAM) are relevant to Emirati patients’ health decisions, particularly in diabetes care [1][3][6][7]. There is no direct contestation within this set, but the scope does not extend to evaluating the clinical efficacy of CAM for diabetes outcomes.

## Open Questions
- Trust and communication
  - What is the prevalence and nature of trust or mistrust in physicians among Emirati patients managing diabetes and obesity, and how does this affect adherence and disclosure (e.g., of CAM use)? The included studies do not quantify this [1][3][6][7].
- Family decision-making
  - How, and to what measurable extent, do family dynamics and collective decision-making shape diet, medication adherence, and CAM/herbal use among Emirati adults with diabetes or obesity? The qualitative lens suggests importance, but quantified effects are lacking in this set [1].
- Clinical impact of CAM/herbal use
  - What are the short- and long-term effects of commonly used herbal/CAM modalities on glycemic control, weight, and cardiometabolic risk among Emirati patients, including potential interactions with prescribed therapies? Existing UAE studies describe patterns but not clinical outcomes [3][6][7].
- Obesity-specific beliefs
  - How do Emirati beliefs about causes and treatments of obesity influence help-seeking, lifestyle change, and uptake of clinical services? The current sources provide population health context without detailing beliefs around obesity management [2][4].
- Implementation within quality management
  - To what degree do UAE health quality management practices operationalize culturally tailored, family-inclusive counseling and clinician training on CAM discussions, and how do these practices affect outcomes? The scoping review maps practices but does not evaluate their impact [2].
