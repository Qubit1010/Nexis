Session structure and running order (with platform mapping)
- Opening and orientation
  - Use a directed chat prompt as the very first audience touch to prime interaction (e.g., “Where are you joining from?” or “What’s your role?”), aligning with the “directed chat prompts” mechanic [4].
  - On Zoom, enable and monitor Chat and Q&A so responses have a visible home; Zoom tutorials explicitly show managing Q&A and enabling polls (you’ll set these up before the session) [7]. Zoom’s advanced tips guidance indicates there are additional configuration options to polish delivery [8]. Zoom’s own events resource frames these as best practices for a professional experience [3].
  - On WebinarJam, the 2026 setup guide takes you “through every screen and every setting decision,” so your opening flow can be configured end-to-end before you go live (the tutorial’s scope covers the live broadcast setup in full) [1].
  - On Riverside, follow the 2026 tutorial to set up your studio and guest flow; the platform’s 4K recording means your live environment is also optimized for later reuse [6].

- Core content with embedded engagement
  - Planted open loops: tease a later reveal or resource early (“I’ll show the template we used near the end”), then close the loop during the offer moment. This is one of the four named mechanics and is specifically positioned to maintain stick rate [4].
  - Strategically timed polls: schedule polls to punctuate content blocks and diagnose audience needs in-session. On Zoom, enabling polls and running them live is part of the 2026 hosting tutorial [7]. EasyWebinar’s guidance emphasizes timing as the lever (not merely having a poll), so place them before transitions or after a key point to regain attention [4].
  - Q&A interludes: Zoom supports managed Q&A sessions; make these short interludes to reset attention and surface objections early [7]. WebinarJam and Riverside setup tutorials are end-to-end, so plan similar interludes within those platforms’ interaction tools as configured during setup [1][6].

- Offer and conversion moment
  - Timed CTA overlays at the offer moment are one of the four mechanics. If your platform supports a visible, timed CTA element, schedule it to coincide with closing your biggest open loop [4]. (Note: the availability and exact implementation of CTA overlays vary by platform; the sources here document the principle and do not enumerate overlay support across Zoom/WebinarJam/Riverside [4][1][7][6].)

- Close and next steps
  - Summarize decisions and outcomes; if using Zoom, leave Q&A open briefly, which is covered as a managed element in the 2026 tutorial [7].
  - Recordings: Riverside’s 4K capture and Magic Clips directly support turning the session into short-form assets afterward [6]. If you hosted elsewhere, export the recording (platform-specific export steps are not detailed in these sources) and apply the same repurposing logic.

- Cross-platform principle: Plant open loops early, time the CTA to coincide with closing those loops, and use interaction points as attention resets rather than add-ons [4].
