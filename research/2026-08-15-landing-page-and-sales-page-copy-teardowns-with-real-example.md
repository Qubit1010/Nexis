## Annotated Breakdowns and Examples (from the sources)
- Example: Burrow’s hero (shipped page; ecommerce)
  - Visual strategy: High-resolution lifestyle imagery communicates product benefits nonverbally [3].
  - Above-the-fold restraint: Minimal copy, links, and CTAs emphasize focus and aesthetics [3].
  - Trade-off to consider: Visual-first can aid immediate comprehension via context; it may defer explicit value claims/objections to below the fold [3].
- Copyhackers Teardown Principle
  - Length calibration: Expand or contract copy to meet the persuasive burden; there is no one-size-fits-all “short vs long” rule [6].
  - Practical implication: Complex, high-friction offers likely need more sections (proof, risk-reversal, FAQs) vs. simple, low-friction offers that can be concise [6].
- CTA/Form Microcopy (Conversion Sciences case study)
  - Principle: Button and form copy should name the desirable end-state or offer; start with action-oriented phrasing (“Request your free consultation now”) rather than generic instructions [7].
  - Practical implication: Treat microcopy as a continuation of your value prop and promise [7].

## Concrete Steps You Can Take (derived from the sources)
- Define page purpose and traffic source
  - Commit to a single desired action and align copy with ad/traffic promise to maintain message match [1].
- Choose a hero strategy to test
  - Variant A: Visual-first, minimal-copy hero (as in Burrow), with high-quality contextual imagery and limited above-the-fold elements [3].
  - Variant B: Copy-led hero that introduces more of your key elements up top when your offer needs verbal persuasion [1][6].
- Calibrate page length to persuasion
  - Expand sections based on complexity, risk, and objections; avoid prescriptive length targets [6].
- Write outcome-oriented form and CTA copy
  - Lead with the offer and the action the prospect wants; avoid generic “Submit”/“Fill out the form” phrasing [7].
- Use a structure to ensure coverage
  - Map your content to a 15-point sales page outline to avoid missing critical elements (e.g., proof, risk reversal, FAQs), even if you later condense it for a shorter landing page [8].
- Follow a documented process
  - Use a step-by-step copywriting approach to move from research to drafts to iteration (details are indicated but not enumerated in the VWO excerpt) [4].
