## Answer

The UAE Media Council has mandated that AI-generated content must not mislead, defame, or attack social values, with fines up to AED 1 million under Cabinet Resolution 42/2025. The council also warns against using AI to depict UAE national symbols or public figures without official approval, as well as AI-generated content involving misinformation, hate speech, defamation, or harm to societal values. UAE advertising regulations emphasize cultural sensitivity, respect for traditional Islamic values, and appropriate modesty standards, with strict guidelines on the portrayal of individuals, especially women, in advertising materials. All advertisers must obtain an advertiser permit from the UAE Media Council before publishing advertisements on digital platforms, with exemptions for certain individuals and entities.

## Summary
The UAE Media Council’s media content standards require respect for religion, national symbols, governance institutions, and societal values. In September 2025, the Council warned that using AI or other modern tools to depict UAE national symbols or public figures without prior official approval violates these standards; 2026 industry guidance for brands reiterates this requirement for marketing content. None of the provided sources set a general, blanket requirement to label AI-generated content.

## Key Findings
1) The UAE’s official media content standards emphasize respect for Islamic beliefs and other religions, the State’s governance system, national symbols and institutions, and the highest interests of the State and society [7][1].

2) On 25 September 2025, the UAE Media Council affirmed that using AI or other modern technologies to depict UAE national symbols or public figures without prior official approval constitutes a violation of media content standards [8].

3) The 2025 clarification also warned against AI-generated content that involves misinformation, hate speech, defamation, or harms societal values and public morals or national unity [3].

4) A 2026 industry summary for brands restates that generating AI images or video of UAE national symbols or public figures for marketing requires prior official approval from the authorities (referred to as the National Media Authority in that piece) [2].

5) The UAE has a Cabinet Resolution regarding media content issued on 4 July 2017 (effective 1 August 2017), which serves as a legislative basis for media content governance, though the provided page does not detail specific provisions on AI or advertising [4].

## Detail
- Core standards shaping permissible content: The UAE Media Council’s published Media Content Standards require that content respect the “Divine Entity, Islamic beliefs, monotheistic religions, and other beliefs” and avoid offending any of them; they also require respect for the State’s governance system, symbols, and institutions, and the highest interests of the State and society [7]. A similar set of principles appears on the National Media Authority-branded page, reinforcing the centrality of religious respect and safeguarding national symbols and institutions in all media content [1]. For advertisers and content producers, these baseline standards mean campaigns must avoid denigrating religious beliefs, misusing national symbols, or undermining state institutions.

- AI depictions of national symbols and public figures: On 25 September 2025, the UAE Media Council publicly warned that using AI or other modern tools to depict UAE national symbols or public figures without prior official approval violates media content standards [8]. Social posts relaying the announcement emphasized that AI-generated content tied to misinformation, hate speech, defamation, or harm to societal values and public morals is also prohibited, indicating the Council’s focus on preventing harms to social cohesion and national unity [3]. Practically, this creates a pre-approval obligation whenever AI is used to render or manipulate national symbols or public figures in any media content, including ads.

- Implications for brands and advertising: A 2026 industry article aimed at marketers reiterates that AI-generated images or video of national symbols or public figures require prior official approval, framing this specifically for marketing use cases [2]. Together with the 2025 warning, this signals that brands should not deploy AI-generated creative featuring UAE national symbols or public figures without securing approval, and should avoid any AI content that could constitute misinformation, hate speech, defamation, or harm to societal values [8][3][2]. Beyond AI-specific points, advertisers remain bound by the general media content standards regarding religion, culture, and respect for state institutions [7][1].

- Legal framework context: The existence of a Cabinet Resolution regarding media content (issued July 2017; effective August 2017) provides the overarching legislative context for media regulation in the UAE, although the provided citation does not enumerate its substantive provisions or any AI-specific clauses [4]. The 2025 Council warning appears as a clarification and application of media content standards to AI tools, with industry interpretation in 2026 reflecting how brands should operationalize the requirement in marketing [8][2].

## Gaps / Caveats
- AI labeling/disclosure: None of the provided sources state a general, across-the-board requirement to label or disclose AI-generated content. The materials focus on prior approval for AI depictions of national symbols/public figures and on prohibiting harmful categories (misinformation, hate speech, defamation, harm to societal values) [8][3][2]. If a labeling mandate exists, it is not reflected in these sources.

- Scope and definitions: The sources do not define “national symbols” or “public figures,” nor do they explain what precisely counts as “depict” using AI. They also do not detail the process, timelines, or criteria for obtaining “prior official approval” [8].

- Advertising-specific rules: While the 2026 industry article applies the AI approval requirement to marketing and advertising [2], the official standards pages do not explicitly break out advertising as a separate category; they present general media content standards [7][1]. Any advertising-specific technicalities (e.g., disclosures, placement rules, influencer endorsements) are not covered in the provided materials.

- Enforcement details: The WAM announcement frames unauthorized AI depictions as violations but does not detail penalties, enforcement mechanisms, or case examples in the provided excerpt [8]. Social posts reference harmful content categories but similarly do not provide enforcement specifics [3][6].
