Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) focuses on AI-powered search engines citing your content; SEO and GEO coexist, with GEO enhancing visibility in AI responses. ## Summary
AI search in 2026 is shifting from ranked lists to direct, cited answers, making Answer Engine Optimization (AEO) and Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) core site-level strategies. The sources agree that GEO means structuring content and brand signals so engines like Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Perplexity retrieve and cite your site; they also outline high-level retrieval pipelines and cross-engine differences but leave important implementation details (e.g., llms.txt, knowledge graphs, crawler controls, query fan-out) largely unspecified [1][2][3][4][5][6][7][8].

## Key Findings

2) AEO vs. GEO: AEO and GEO are presented together as complementary approaches to get cited by Google AI Overviews and by chat-style engines (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity) [4].  
3) Generative search changes discovery: Users get direct answers and recommendations instead of lists of links, reshaping brand visibility dynamics [3][7].

## Detail
- What GEO is and why it matters  
  - Multiple guides define GEO as organizing site content and brand signals so that AI engines retrieve and cite the site within generated answers [1][2][3][8]. This aligns with the observed shift toward direct-answer experiences where users see synthesized responses with cited sources rather than classic SERPs [3][7].  
  - Practically, GEO’s outcome metric is citation/selection by engines like ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews [1][2][4][8].

- AEO vs. GEO at the site level  
  - AEO and GEO are presented together as frameworks focused on getting your site cited across Google AI Overviews and chat-style assistants [4]. The emphasis is site-level preparedness to be selected, rather than only page-level ranking in traditional SEO [4].  
  - The overlap: both aim at inclusion in AI-generated answers; the difference in emphasis is that GEO spans multiple engines and their differing retrieval/citation behaviors, while AEO explicitly includes Google’s AI Overviews context [4].

- How engines pick sources (selection, retrieval, and citation differences)  
  - Comparative view: A cross-platform comparison notes that engines differ in how they retrieve content, evaluate it, and decide what to cite; these differences directly influence visibility prospects by platform [5].  
  - Pipeline view: A five-stage retrieval pipeline is outlined, naming the underlying indexes engines rely on (Bing, Sonar, Google, live fetch), and pointing to sub-document indexing as a factor with implications for how content chunks (e.g., paragraphs) are treated during retrieval [6].  
  - Implications: Because engines use different indexes and retrieval strategies, a single optimization approach is unlikely to generalize; visibility depends on aligning with each engine’s retrieval/evaluation/citation process [5][6].

- Google AI Overviews and inclusion  
  - Guides that cover AEO and GEO include Google AI Overviews as a destination for citations; success is framed as being selected and linked within the overview answer unit [4].  
  - Since each engine’s retrieval/evaluation differs, inclusion in AI Overviews should be addressed within that engine’s pipeline rather than assuming universal rules [5][6].

- Brand signals as a visibility lever  
  - GEO explicitly includes brand signals as part of what to structure so engines will select and cite a site [1]. This widens the scope beyond just on-page content and suggests that off-page indicators tied to brand may be relevant to selection [1].  
  - However, the sources here do not enumerate specific “brand mention” tactics or measurement; they simply affirm the importance of brand signals within GEO [1].

  - Entity SEO and knowledge graphs: There is no explicit guidance on entity optimization or building/aligning with knowledge graphs in these sources [1][2][3][4][5][6][7][8].  
  - AI crawler access: No concrete recommendations on allowing/controlling AI crawler access (e.g., crawler identification, robots-like directives) appear in this set [1][2][3][4][5][6][7][8].  
  - Query fan-out mechanics: The materials do not explain how engines fan out queries to multiple retrieval paths or indexes, nor how to optimize for such behavior [1][2][3][4][5][6][7][8].  
  - Engine-specific citation rules: While differences are acknowledged, the exact citation criteria, thresholds, or trust signals per engine are not spelled out in these references [5][6].
