## Detail
What Instagram officially says about ranking and distribution
- Multiple ranking systems, not one algorithm. Instagram explains that it does not have a single algorithm; instead, it uses different ranking systems for different parts of the app (Feed, Stories, Explore, Reels) tailored to how people use each surface [2]. 
- Core signals and predictions. For each surface, Instagram models your likelihood of engaging with a given piece of content using signals including your activity on Instagram, your history of interactions with the account that posted, information about the post (e.g., recency, popularity), and information about the person who posted. These signals inform predictions like whether you’ll like, comment, or watch the content, and posts are ordered accordingly [2].
- Feed and Stories. These surfaces primarily show content from people you follow. Instagram emphasizes signals related to recency and your relationship with the poster (e.g., your history of interactions), plus your broader activity patterns, to predict what you’ll care about most in the moment [2].
- Explore. Explore is designed to help you discover new content from accounts you don’t yet follow. Here, Instagram pays more attention to signals around the content’s overall popularity and your past behavior with similar content, while still considering your history with the poster if any exists [2].
- Reels. Reels focuses on entertainment and discovery. The system predicts whether you’ll find a reel entertaining, watch it through, and engage with it (e.g., liking), and ranks accordingly. This surface is geared toward introducing content beyond your follow graph [2].

Practical teardown: applying Instagram’s stated signals
Below, each walkthrough derives directly from the categories of signals and predictions Instagram describes and shows how a practitioner would operationalize them. Because Instagram does not publish weights or guarantees in the provided sources, these are structured as alignment steps rather than “hacks” or fixed thresholds [2][3].

- Feed/Stories alignment
  1) Recency: Plan timely posts and Stories for when your audience is active, since Instagram considers how recent a post is among its signals for these surfaces [2]. Use your account’s Insights to identify when followers are active, then schedule accordingly [3].
  2) Relationship: Systematically reply to DMs and comments and encourage lightweight interactions (e.g., Story replies, polls). Instagram states it looks at your history of interactions with an account to predict interest, so consistently engaging your core audience can strengthen these relationship signals over time [2].
  3) Interest/activity fit: Audit your past posts to see what topics and formats your followers interacted with most (via Insights), and emphasize those themes in upcoming Feed posts and Stories to align with the “your activity” and “information about the post” signals Instagram says it uses [2][3].

- Explore alignment
  1) Content‑level quality/popularity: Instagram says Explore emphasizes post‑level popularity and your past behavior with similar content [2]. Publish posts designed to earn saves/shares from your existing audience (as a proxy for broader appeal), then monitor Insights for saves/shares on those posts [3].
  2) Topical adjacency: Cluster posts around clear topics so that people who engage with similar content are more likely to be predicted to engage with yours. This lines up with Instagram’s description that it uses your activity and post information to predict interest in Explore [2].

- Reels alignment
  1) Completion and entertainment: Instagram says Reels predictions include whether you’ll watch to the end and find it entertaining [2]. Keep Reels tight and engaging early to improve completion likelihood; monitor retention in Insights to iterate [3].
  2) Discovery beyond followers: Reels aims to introduce content from accounts users don’t follow [2]. Calibrate Reels for broad appeal (clear hooks, accessible topics) and track reach to non-followers in Insights to see whether your creative adjustments align with the system’s predictions [3].
