## Detail

Section order that works (and why)
- Opener that “tells a story” and centers the reader’s benefit: The design guidance emphasizes readability and storytelling focused on the audience; use a short opening that frames what the reader will get and why it matters before you ask for action [2].  
- Scannable content blocks with a single action each: Designmodo’s 2026 guidance notes that the best newsletters keep one clear action per block and use a repeatable HTML template. This imposes hierarchy, prevents overload, and maps naturally to either a single deep-dive (one main block) or a curated roundup (several blocks) [3].  
- Clear CTAs: Calls to action are explicitly listed as a core element of effective newsletter design; place them where the decision is made—within or just after each block, and include a primary CTA for the issue [2].  
- Consistent template: Use one repeatable HTML template so readers recognize structure and can scan faster; Mailchimp provides formatting tips/templates to implement this, and Designmodo frames the template as a shared trait of top performers [3][8].

How to open an issue (step-by-step technique)
- Step 1: State the promise in a sentence or two—what new value this issue delivers. This follows the “tells a story” and “focuses on the audience” guidance [2].  
- Step 2: Transition immediately to the first action-oriented block. Keep the block singular in purpose (“one clear action”) and make the CTA explicit [3].  
- Step 3: If using multiple blocks, maintain the same micro-structure per block (title → 1–3 tight sentences → one CTA), preserving readability and scannability as emphasized in the design tutorial and formatting tips [2][8].  
- Step 4: Close with a concise summary or sign-off and one primary CTA (e.g., reply, share, or try the featured resource), aligning to “includes CTAs” and “focuses on the audience” [2].

Single-topic vs roundup formats
- Both work; your format choice is a “silent driver of engagement,” per the 2026 formats overview. Select the structure that best matches your goal for a given issue: depth (single-topic) or breadth (roundup) [1].  
- For single-topic issues: Lean into storytelling and readability (the “tells a story” guidance), using a single, well-developed block and a clear primary CTA [2][3].  
- For roundup issues: Use multiple discrete blocks, each with one clear action (Designmodo’s pattern), and a consistent layout so readers can quickly scan and choose what’s relevant [3][8].
