## Answer

In New York and New Jersey, boutique architecture firms like Ying Li Architect and Sugarhouse Design & Architecture offer specialized services for both residential and commercial projects. These firms emphasize integrated design and high-quality execution.

## Summary
The New York–New Jersey architecture market is intensely competitive and diverse, spanning global multi-practice firms, regional full-service practices, and specialists in multifamily residential, commercial, and landscape/site planning. New York City, in particular, concentrates top-tier players and a large volume of firms, while New Jersey and Upstate New York feature established regional competitors in commercial and community-focused work [1][2][3][5][6][7][8].

## Key Findings
1) New York has exceptional density and visibility of architecture talent, with 20,000+ licensed architects and 1,000+ firms statewide; curated “best-of” lists highlight top-tier NYC players, underscoring intense competition [1].  
2) Large global competitors operate from NYC, notably Gensler, whose New York office is the firm’s largest and delivers marquee projects across sectors including aviation (JFK’s New Terminal One), cultural (Jackie Robinson Museum), and corporate/tech workplaces (IBM NYC Flagship; Google at St. John’s) [2].  
3) Multifamily residential is a defined specialization: Handel Architects explicitly positions itself as a specialist in multifamily residential, also delivering mixed-use, office, adaptive reuse, and context-driven work [4].  
4) In New Jersey, established regional competitors include SSP Architects (commercial-focused; “spaces to learn, work, and play”) and LAN Associates (self-described leading NJ architecture firm since 1965), indicating strong local options for commercial and community projects [3][5].  
5) Beyond NYC, Upstate New York competition includes full-service architecture/engineering (A/E) practices focused on commercial properties, such as Beardsley, emphasizing sustainable and integrated design services [6].  
6) Landscape architecture and site-planning specialists are active and often integral to project teams: MKW + Associates (NJ) and New York firms like Hollander Design (serving private homeowners to institutions) signal competition and collaboration in site, landscape, and urban design scopes adjacent to residential/commercial architecture [7][8].  
7) The available sources do not classify firms by size or “boutique” status, nor do they provide market share, fee structures, or detailed sector splits by geography; competitive segmentation by firm size therefore cannot be documented from these materials [1][2][3][4][5][6][7][8].

## Detail
New York City competitive intensity  
- Architizer frames New York as “arguably the epicenter” of U.S. architecture, citing 20,000+ licensed architects and 1,000+ firms statewide and curating a list of 35 top NYC firms. The ecosystem also includes interior designers, landscape architects, and engineers, reflecting a multidisciplinary marketplace where architecture firms compete and collaborate across specialties [1]. This concentration implies elevated competition for both residential (e.g., multifamily) and commercial commissions in NYC.

Large multi-practice competitor footprint in NYC  
- Gensler’s New York office is the firm’s largest, underscoring deep capacity and breadth. Its portfolio in NYC includes high-profile, complex work across multiple sectors—aviation (New Terminal One at JFK), cultural (Jackie Robinson Museum), and major workplace/tech projects (IBM NYC Flagship; Google at St. John’s)—illustrating the scale and range of services large firms bring to commercial and civic clients in the city [2]. For boutique practices, such firms represent formidable competition on large, high-visibility commercial assignments.

Residential specialization signals  
- Handel Architects explicitly identifies as a specialist in multifamily residential design, while also highlighting mixed-use, office, adaptive reuse, and context-sensitive approaches aimed at positive urban and social change [4]. This positioning indicates that, within NYC’s crowded field, specialization in multifamily and mixed-use is a distinct competitive lane for residential-focused work [4]. Although the source does not specify geography, the specialization itself shapes the competitive set for residential/mixed-use pursuits.

Regional competitive dynamics in New Jersey  
- SSP Architects presents as a New Jersey commercial architecture firm with a focus on “spaces to learn, work, and play,” emphasizing an end-to-end partnership from concept through construction. This suggests strong positioning in education, workplace, and recreational/community facilities within NJ’s commercial and institutional sectors [3].  
- LAN Associates describes itself as a leading New Jersey architecture firm since 1965, with a mission centered on transforming the built environment of local communities. This longevity and regional focus position LAN as a well-established competitor across community and commercial projects in NJ [5].  
Together, SSP and LAN reflect a competitive field of NJ-based firms with local knowledge and full-service delivery models in commercial/community markets [3][5].

Upstate New York full-service A/E competition  
- Beardsley targets commercial architecture and engineering for Upstate New York, emphasizing sustainable, full-service solutions that integrate architecture, engineering, and landscape architecture. This integrated model is a competitive differentiator for regional commercial projects outside NYC’s core [6].

Landscape and site-planning specialists influencing residential/commercial bids  
- MKW + Associates, a Rutherford, NJ firm, offers landscape architecture, site planning, and urban design services and is an award-winning practice—capabilities that frequently intersect with both residential and commercial developments, influencing site strategies and design coordination in the NJ market [7].  
- In New York, lists of “best firms” include landscape architecture specialists such as Hollander Design, which serves a wide range of clients from private homeowners to institutions—covering high-end residential landscapes as well as institutional work—indicating that landscape-focused firms are material competitors and collaborators in shaping overall project teams and outcomes [8].  
These adjacent disciplines add competitive complexity to residential and commercial pursuits by broadening the set of specialist firms vying for scope on project teams [7][8].

Overall competitive picture for boutique positioning  
- The sources collectively indicate a landscape where:  
  - NYC hosts large, multi-practice firms with marquee portfolios [2] and a dense field of top-tier competitors [1].  
  - Residential competition includes firms specializing in multifamily and mixed-use [4].  
  - NJ and Upstate NY markets feature established, regional full-service practices in commercial/community sectors [3][5][6].  
  - Landscape/site-planning specialists play a notable role alongside architecture firms in both states [7][8].  
While “boutique” status is not defined, smaller or specialist practices would be competing against both global players for complex urban commissions and established regional firms with local relationships for commercial/community work [1][2][3][5][6].

- The “best-of” lists are editorial/curated; methodologies, inclusion criteria, and comprehensiveness are not provided, so they indicate visibility rather than market share or performance metrics [1][8].  
- Residential coverage is limited to a multifamily specialist signal (Handel) and landscape specialists; the sources do not provide detail on NJ-focused residential architecture boutiques or on single-family residential architects in NY/NJ [4][7][8].
