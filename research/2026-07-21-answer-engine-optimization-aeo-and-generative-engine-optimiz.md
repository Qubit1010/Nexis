## Summary
Across 2023–2026, the emerging consensus in Answer/Generative Engine Optimization (AEO/GEO) is that getting cited by AI answer engines depends less on traditional Google rank and more on aligning content to user intent and making answers structurally easy to extract and attribute. Structural features on the page shape whether models cite you, not just whether your text influences their answers. Multi‑platform evidence indicates query intent and rank act jointly, with intent often the stronger signal. Benchmarking work shows that GEO tactics can materially lift visibility—up to 40% in controlled evaluations—though real‑world referral analytics must be disentangled from platform growth. ## Key Findings
- Field overview and definitions (GEO vs. SEO/AEO): A 2026 critical survey defines GEO as practices that increase a source’s presence in generative answers and situates GEO as distinct from traditional link‑rank SEO, synthesizing methods published 2023–2026 [1].
- Disentangling optimization impact from platform growth: A log‑based natural experiment on ChatGPT referral traffic shows that observed traffic changes can be driven by platform growth rather than optimization alone, cautioning against attributing gains to AEO without proper controls [2].
- Structure shapes citation behavior: An arXiv preprint demonstrates that page‑level structural features measurably shape whether AI engines cite a source, indicating that explicit, modular structures (e.g., clearly labeled sections) can raise citation likelihood beyond unstructured prose with the same content [3].
- Query intent outranks Google position as a predictor: A 2026 preprint finds that query intent is a stronger predictor of AI citation than a page’s Google rank, implying that tailoring pages to dominant intents for a topic is critical for being cited by AI engines [4].
- From “contribution” to “citation”: A method paper argues many GEO evaluations optimize for contribution (how much a document influences a model’s answer) rather than actual citation; it proposes diagnostic and repair techniques aimed at fixing “citation failures,” i.e., when a source influences content but is not cited [5].
- The “SEO floor”: An observational study measures the Google‑rank distribution of AI‑cited pages and shows that citations are not confined to top Google results, supporting the view that GEO is not reducible to classic SEO and that lower‑ranked pages can still be cited by AI engines [6].
- Joint effects across platforms: A multi‑platform study reports that query intent and Google rank jointly predict AI citations, indicating that both factors matter and that cross‑engine strategies should balance intent alignment with baseline search visibility [7].
- Measurement and effect sizes: A benchmarking paper introduces an open instrument/protocol (GEO‑bench) and reports that proposed GEO methods can boost generative visibility by up to 40% across diverse queries, providing a measurable upside to structured, GEO‑targeted interventions [8].

Actionable implications supported by the studies:
