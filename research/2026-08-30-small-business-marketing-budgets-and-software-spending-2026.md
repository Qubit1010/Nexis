# small business marketing budgets and software spending 2026: what percentage of revenue SMBs spend on marketing, what marketing tools SMBs buy and cancel, DIY versus agency versus freelancer, SMB marketing software churn

*mode: general | depth: deep | 2026-08-30*

---

## Answer

Small businesses typically spend 7-12% of revenue on marketing in 2026, often using tools like Google Ads and email marketing. SMBs often cancel marketing software that doesn't deliver ROI. Agencies and freelancers are preferred for complex campaigns, while DIY approaches are common for basic needs.

## Summary
Across the available 2026 guidance, small businesses are generally advised to allocate roughly 5–12% of revenue to marketing, with the lower end appropriate for maintenance and the higher end for growth pushes. The provided sources do not offer reliable, quantitative evidence on SMB marketing software purchases and cancellations, DIY vs agency vs freelancer mix, or software churn.

## Key Findings
1) Recommended SMB marketing budget as a share of revenue in 2026 clusters around 5–12%, with 5–7% as a conservative baseline and 7–12% for growth initiatives [7]; a frequently cited benchmark is 7–8% of gross revenue for established businesses under $5M (attributed to the U.S. SBA) [3].

2) General “how much to spend” resources emphasize tailoring the budget to revenue, market, and goals rather than a single percentage target [2][4][6].

3) Advertising spend is only a subset of total marketing: one SBA blog post (2019) cites an average of 1.08% of revenues spent on advertising, which should not be conflated with full marketing budgets or 2026 norms [8].

4) B2B-specific 2026 budget benchmarks exist but are not SMB-specific; they may be directional context rather than direct SMB comparators [5].

5) The provided sources do not furnish empirical data on: which marketing software/tools SMBs most commonly buy or cancel, DIY vs agency vs freelancer usage patterns, or SMB marketing software churn rates [1][2][3][4][5][6][7][8].

## Detail
Percentage of revenue benchmarks (2026):
- Multiple 2026-oriented guides converge on a range rather than a single point estimate. One source recommends roughly 5–7% of gross revenue for many small businesses, with 7–12% for those in “growth mode” [7]. Another cites the widely referenced SBA guideline of 7–8% of gross revenue for established firms under $5M, framing it as a practical benchmark rather than a hard rule [3].
- Additional resources stress context over rigid targets. Guidance from Mercury (2026) and Mailchimp underscores building a budget around business goals, stage, and channel mix, not just a fixed percentage [2][4]. BDC similarly notes the right spend depends on a firm’s revenues, market, and target audience [6].

Advertising vs. total marketing:
- The SBA blog’s cited statistic—average advertising at 1.08% of revenues—reflects only advertising (and from 2019), not a full marketing budget. It is therefore not directly comparable to 2026 marketing-budget guidelines and should be used cautiously and only for historical context [8].

B2B context:
- Directive’s 2026 B2B budget benchmarks provide contemporary, channel-focused perspective for business marketers, but they are not SMB-specific and may reflect different spend profiles and goals than typical small businesses. As such, they are better used as directional context than as SMB budget norms [5].
