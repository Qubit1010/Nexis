## Key Findings
- Microcelebrity: definition and core practices
  - Microcelebrity is being well known to a niche audience and involves presenting oneself as a public persona, building affective ties, and treating followers as fans; it is tightly linked to self-branding online [1]. The sources describe practices and logics but do not report numerical effect sizes [1].

- Self-branding as entrepreneurial labor and commodification of the self
  - Self-branding and micro-celebrity are co-constitutive; influencers monetize identity and image, crafting “intimate” exchanges that audiences read as authentic. This is documented as a socio-cultural shift rather than quantified performance change [5], [7]. No numerical effect sizes are reported in these sources [5], [7].

- Perceived authenticity via “intimate exchange” and backstage access
  - Influencers’ profiles give followers access to what appears to be “genuine, raw” aspects of life; this “perceived authenticity” is cultivated and monetized through self-branding practices [7]. The source emphasizes perception and craft rather than measured authenticity indices; no effect sizes are reported [7].

- Credibility and self-presentation of “Instafamous” micro-celebrities
  - Firms increasingly use micro-celebrities for endorsements while credibility concerns persist. Credibility is tied to self-presentation choices and perceived authenticity in followers’ eyes [8]. The cited work focuses on credibility themes; it does not provide quantitative endorsement effectiveness or effect sizes in this set [8].

- “#NoFilter” and authenticity signaling on Instagram
  - Micro-celebrities deploy self-branding practices that specifically perform authenticity (e.g., aesthetic and textual cues such as “no filter”). The study situates these tactics within classic impression management/presentation-of-self frameworks, but the provided material does not report numerical engagement lifts or causal effects [2].

- Conceptual foundations: presentation of self and online personal branding processes
  - The literature anchors in Goffman’s presentation-of-self and related impression management concepts, adapted to platform logics. Online personal branding is positioned as the strategic management of identity signals to convey professional value, credibility, and competence [2], [3]. These sources outline processes rather than reporting quantitative outcomes [2], [3].
