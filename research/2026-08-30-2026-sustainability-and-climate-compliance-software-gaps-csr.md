# 2026 sustainability and climate compliance software gaps: CSRD omnibus, scope 3 emissions, supply chain due diligence CSDDD, SMB carbon accounting, EPR packaging regulation, unmet needs companies pay consultants for

*mode: general | depth: deep | 2026-08-30*

---

## Answer

Companies face gaps in software for CSRD compliance, scope 3 emissions, and supply chain due diligence; they often hire consultants for tailored solutions. EPR packaging regulation overlaps with carbon accounting. SMB carbon accounting and regulatory compliance remain unmet needs.

## Summary
In 2026 the EU “Omnibus” reset narrowed who must report under CSRD and revised the European Sustainability Reporting Standards, while the broader EU sustainability regime emphasized voluntary leadership and cross-framework alignment. Across CSRD/CDP/SEC integration and the operational overlap between packaging EPR data and Scope 3 carbon accounting, the sources point to complex, cross-cutting data and reporting needs; these imply software gaps in scoping, standards updates, multi-framework mapping, and EPR–Scope 3 data pipelines [4][1][3][6][2][8][7].

## Key Findings
1) CSRD scope was materially reduced by the EU Omnibus in 2026, with sources describing ~80% fewer companies remaining in scope and emphasizing that “who must report and how much” changed, along with revised ESRS [4][1][3].  
2) The Omnibus narrowed the focus to larger companies and left fewer entities subject to mandatory reporting and due diligence; the Council’s final approval for Omnibus I came on 24 February 2026 [7].  
3) The regulatory “reset” lowered the mandatory bar but raised the stakes for voluntary leadership, shifting attention to harmonized reporting and strategy beyond minimum compliance [6].  
4) 2026 guidance explicitly focuses on integrating CDP, CSRD, and SEC climate disclosures via a compliance roadmap, underscoring the need to reconcile frameworks in parallel [2].  
5) Packaging EPR compliance requires granular material/weight/recyclability/supply-volume data that substantially overlaps with the data needed for Scope 3 emissions accounting under the GHG Protocol, creating an operational intersection between EPR and carbon accounting [8].  
6) CSRD is in force and undergoing continued 2026 changes; stakeholders must track updates across the evolving EU sustainability rule set [5][3].  
7) Implications for software (inferred from the sources):  
   - Dynamic scoping and eligibility assessment must reflect the Omnibus reduction and revised ESRS [4][3][1].  
   - Multi-framework mapping and workflow support are needed to integrate CDP, CSRD, and SEC disclosures alongside growing voluntary leadership aims [2][6].  
   - EPR–Scope 3 data integration is a clear data-management need given the strong overlap in required inputs [8].  
   - Due diligence applicability narrowed, but the sources provide limited detail on operational software needs for supply chain due diligence under the reset [7].  

## Detail
CSRD after the 2026 Omnibus
- Multiple sources state that the EU Omnibus materially reduced the scope of CSRD; one reports that about 80% of companies that were previously in scope are now exempt and that the package entered into force on 19 March 2026 [4]. Another describes how the Omnibus “reshaped who must report and how much” and outlines the current state of CSRD compliance in 2026 [1]. Deloitte flags “Omnibus Legislative Developments and Revised European Sustainability Reporting Standards,” indicating that not only scoping but also the content standards were updated in 2026 [3].  
- Anthesis notes “a narrower focus on larger companies and fewer entities subject to mandatory due diligence and reporting,” and confirms the Council’s final approval for Omnibus I on 24 February 2026 [7].  
- Collectively, these changes imply that software needs to:  
  - Recalculate scoping and applicability under the post-Omnibus regime; and  
  - Update content models and controls to reflect revised ESRS [4][3][1][7].  

Voluntary leadership and cross-framework alignment
- ISS Corporate describes 2026 as a “reset” that “lowered the regulatory bar – but raised the stakes for voluntary leadership,” meaning organizations that want resilience and future-proofing must go beyond minimums [6].  
- In parallel, AIGovHub provides a 2026 roadmap to integrate CDP, CSRD, and SEC climate disclosures, underscoring the practical need to align multiple regimes in the same reporting cycle [2].  
- From a software perspective, these points together suggest demand for:  
  - Common data models and mappings across frameworks (CDP, CSRD, SEC); and  
  - Workflows that allow concurrent preparation, gap analysis, and evidence management across regimes, supporting both compliance and voluntary leadership aims [2][6].  

EPR packaging regulation and Scope 3 emissions
- ASUENE highlights that “one of the most underrecognized aspects of the EPR compliance wave is its overlap with corporate carbon accounting,” specifically that the packaging data producers must collect for EPR (materials, weights, recyclability, volumes) is “substantially the same” as that required for accurate Scope 3 accounting [8].  
- This directly suggests a software/data gap: organizations benefit from unified data pipelines that feed both packaging EPR compliance and Scope 3 inventories, minimizing duplication and inconsistencies [8].  

Supply chain due diligence under the reset
- Anthesis indicates “fewer entities [are] subject to mandatory due diligence and reporting” post-Omnibus [7]. ISS’s “lower bar” language similarly frames a reduced mandatory scope in 2026 [6].  
- The sources do not provide detailed, operational requirements for due diligence tooling under the new scope, but they establish that applicability has narrowed, implying that the organizations still in scope will need to interpret updated obligations while others may opt for voluntary approaches [7][6].  

Ongoing regulatory evolution
- Footprint Intelligence emphasizes that CSRD is in force, while Deloitte’s 2026 “revised ESRS” message signals ongoing updates that compliance teams must track [5][3].  
- These moving parts suggest software needs for version control of requirements, auditability of changes, and configurable templates to adapt to updated ESRS and multi-regime disclosures [5][3][2].  

Unmet needs companies pay consultants for
- The sources do not state which activities companies hire consultants for or how much they spend. They do, however, center on areas that typically require expert interpretation: scoping under the Omnibus [4][1], updates to ESRS [3], integrating CDP/CSRD/SEC [2], and connecting EPR packaging with Scope 3 accounting [8]. The fact that these are the focus of 2026 guides and analyses implies they are pain points, but the sources do not quantify or explicitly attribute them to consultant engagements [4][1][3][2][8].

## Gaps / Caveats
- The sources do not quantify software capability gaps, adoption rates, or market coverage; they focus on regulatory changes and integration guidance [1][2][3][4][5][6][7][8].  
- SMB carbon accounting is not explicitly covered; the closest signal is that many previously in-scope entities are now exempt, and voluntary leadership becomes more important, but the sources do not discuss SMB-specific needs or tooling [4][6][7].  
- For supply chain due diligence (CSDDD context), the sources mention that fewer entities are subject to mandatory due diligence, but provide limited operational detail on requirements or software functionalities needed under the 2026 reset [7].  
- None of the sources specify which unmet needs companies pay consultants for or provide examples of consultant scopes of work; any such assertions would be speculative without additional evidence [1][2][3][4][5][6][7][8].
