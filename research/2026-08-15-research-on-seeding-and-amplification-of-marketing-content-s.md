## Answer

Targeting hubs and bridges in viral marketing yields larger cascades than fringes; earned exposure often outperforms paid in media mix modeling; empirical studies show hubs can generate up to eight times more referrals than other strategies.

## Summary
Across the provided peer‑reviewed sources, the strongest convergent finding is that seeding initial exposure with individuals who are network “hubs” (high degree centrality) and/or “bridges” (high betweenness centrality) yields larger diffusion than alternative strategies tested, based on two small field experiments and one real-life viral marketing campaign reported in the same research stream. Prior literature was not unanimous, however: reviews note recommendations ranging from hubs to periphery (“fringe”) to bridges. The materials here do not provide quantitative media‑mix or paid‑vs‑earned attribution, nor field tests directly comparing organic reach to paid amplification of the same asset, nor evidence on syndication/duplicate distribution effects. Where available, we report the specific counts and named frameworks that are stated in the sources and preserve disagreement.

## Key Findings
- Literature recommendations conflict on who to seed:
  - A review in the Journal of Marketing article states that “Four studies recommend seeding hubs, three recommend fringes, and one recommends bridges,” and analyzes why these discrepant recommendations can arise [2].
- Empirical comparison (field experiments + live campaign) of seeding strategies:
  - The research program “compares four seeding strategies in two complementary small-scale field experiments, as well as in one real-life viral marketing campaign,” providing direct, empirical contrasts among strategies [3].
  - Instructional summary from the same research stream: “High-degree and high-betweenness strategies work best for viral marketing campaigns,” i.e., targeting hubs and bridges outperforms alternatives evaluated in the experiments/campaign [4].
  - The working paper and conference versions document the same study lineage and focus—“Seeding Strategies for Viral Marketing: An Empirical Comparison”—consistent with the above conclusions (academic authors; details align with [3][4]) [5][6].
- Frameworks/named strategies explicitly referenced:
  - Degree centrality (“hubs”) and betweenness centrality (“bridges”) are the strategies identified as top performers in the empirical comparisons [4][6].
  - “Fringe” (peripheral/low‑degree) seeding is discussed in the literature review as an alternative some prior studies endorse [2].
- Mechanistic rationale for seeding (conceptual, not quantified here):
  - A peer‑reviewed study on brand spreading in complex social networks defines seeding as selecting “a small number of consumers (seeding users)” early, with diffusion driven by interpersonal communication; this underscores why network position matters for efficiency of spread, though it does not, in the provided excerpt, report comparative effect sizes among specific centrality‑based strategies [8].
- Practitioner/agency perspective (non‑peer‑reviewed):
  - A marketing blog summarizes common targeting options—hubs, bridges, and fringes—and cautions that hubs “may not be optimal” in some cases (e.g., overload or reduced credibility), reflecting practitioner opinion rather than controlled tests in these materials [7].
- Scope limits in these sources (no direct quantitative evidence here):

We preserve this tension rather than declaring a universal winner.
