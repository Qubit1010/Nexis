Across 2026 sources, typical SEO retainers span roughly $1,500–$15,000+ per month depending on client size and complexity; small businesses commonly pay $2,500–$5,000, mid‑market $5,000–$10,000, and enterprise often exceeds $10,000, with averages near $2,900–$3,500/month reported. ## Detail
Retainers and segment benchmarks
- Range consensus: Multiple sources place common monthly retainers squarely in the low four figures, widening to high four or low five figures as complexity and scale rise. The Stacc summarizes retainers as $1,500–$10,000+ and also lists hourly, project, and value‑based approaches [1]. Digital Applied places the overall retainer range at $1,500–$15,000+, with a reported median of $3,500/month and segment guidance: small business $2,500–$5,000 and mid‑market $5,000–$10,000 [2]. GoodFirms aligns by noting most companies charge $1,500–$5,000, with enterprise and regulated sectors frequently paying $5,000+/month [4]. A third benchmark cites an average cost around $2,917/month (agencies closer to ~$3,200/month), consistent with the central tendency in [2][4] [7].
- Enterprise variance: Sources signal upward elasticity at the top end: The Stacc’s “$10,000+” language and Digital Applied’s “$15,000+” ceiling imply enterprise retainers often surpass $10,000, while GoodFirms sets a lower enterprise floor at $5,000+. This suggests differing definitions of “enterprise” and scope breadth across markets [1][2][4].

Pricing models and their use
- Models in use: Retainers, hourly (range $75–$300), project fees, and value‑based pricing are all active options in 2026 per The Stacc [1]. Digital Applied quantifies the prevailing specialist rate at ~$150/hr, which sits near the midpoint of The Stacc’s range [2]. GoodFirms reinforces the modal retainer band ($1,500–$5,000/month) and the tendency for higher budgets in complex or regulated contexts [4].
- Strategy framing: While the SEO‑specific sources focus on concrete ranges and models, general pricing references (Salesforce, Coursera, Wikipedia) clarify the broader toolkit—cost‑plus, competitive, skimming, penetration, and value‑based—underscoring that model selection should balance competitiveness, value, and profitability [5][6][3]. The Stacc’s “when to use each model” language indicates agencies align pricing structure with engagement scope and value, though it does not provide SOW templates or scoping checklists [1].
