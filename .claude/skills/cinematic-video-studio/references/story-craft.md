# Story craft: structure, pacing, lines

## Structures that worked (CURE, client-approved)

- **Two timelines, one idea** (Pearl Diver): a heritage past and a present-day science world
  cut together with match cuts (pearl → planet, thread → chain, held breath → exhale). A
  heartbeat or other sound carries across the cut. Best for an awe-inspiring explainer.
- **Question → expert → self-discovery** (Majlis Question): an everyday moment raises an honest
  question, nobody pretends to know, they ask the expert (a placeholder slot if the expert must
  be real), then the elder makes the connection herself and gets the last word. Best for a series format.
- **Heirloom bridge** (Three Generations): a family object (a pearl necklace in a mandoos) is the
  physical metaphor. Push into the object, into the science, back out, and pass it down. Best
  for family and values themes.

- **Comedy, elder gets the last word** (Grandma vs the Phone, planned): escalation in threes (three
  pings, three fake ads, cuts tightening each time), a deadpan look to camera, the music dropping
  out (`score.mute`) for the punchline, and a final button after the line. The joke is always on
  the bad information, never on the elder. Fake ads or claims being mocked are edit graphics
  (never generated into the footage), labelled "example of a misleading ad", and flagged for compliance.
- **Quiz** (Myth or Fact, planned): numbered statement cards, a stamp SFX on each verdict,
  answers from the expert slot, and a "take the full quiz" end-card CTA. If it was the fast,
  cheap variation, keep a ~20s cut of it too.

Common moves:
- Open on texture, not exposition: a slow-motion pour, a macro of the hero object.
- Put the title over shot 1 (thin type, wide letter spacing), not on a separate card.
- One central metaphor carried through every shot.
- End on warmth, a wide shot with laughter, then the end card.

## Pacing

- 45-65s total, 8-12 shots, each 4-6s (Kling clips are 6s; use up to about 5.8s of each).
- Cuts between beats in the same world; dissolves (0.5-1.2s) when changing worlds or scale; a
  dip-to-white for a sunlight match cut.
- Shots can be slowed (`slow: 0.6-0.9`) to stretch a calm clip.

## Lines

- Arabic placeholder narration runs about 1.6 words per second including pauses. Budget each
  line to its shot, and remember lines may run across a cut (captions follow the voice, not the shot).
- One line per voice-audio file, so timing stays editable.
- Keep science claims to one plain sentence each, and list them for Medical Director sign-off.
- Children: no AI child voice. Let their faces carry the moment.

## Things that must be real in the final

Doctors, staff and real patients are never AI. Mark their slot in the concept cut with a
`placeholder` card ("CURE doctor answer, filmed at CURE"), show the line as text, and put
footage the expert would talk over behind it (micro-world shots work well). If the slot must be
audible for the concept, use a narrator voice, with the card saying "placeholder narrator
voice, not a doctor".

## No lip sync

Kling clips are silent and voices are added later, so mouths never match words. Keep on-camera
lines short, let the line land on a reaction or a cutaway, and prompt "minimal mouth movement"
for to-camera shots. Narration and voice-over hide this best.

## A series, not a film

When a client has several films, read the other films' `script.md` first:
- **Vary openers, settings and casts:** CURE already has two gahwa-pour openings.
- **Keep the characters' canon consistent:** who is alive, who is related to whom. Record it in
  the profile's `canon`.
- **Offer a short cutdown** (15-30s) from the same assets when the long version replaces
  something that was meant to be quick.

## Reusing footage

Generic science or scenic shots can serve several films and give a series one look (CURE reuses
the Pearl Diver micro-world clips). Note it in the review flags, because it repeats when films
play back to back.
