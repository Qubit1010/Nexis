# Review Rubric — auditing an existing brand strategy

Audit mode. The client has a brand deck, a strategy document, a set of statements, or just a
website that implies a position. The job is to say what is load-bearing, what is decoration,
and what is missing, then fix the pieces worth fixing.

**This rubric is ours.** There is no validated brand-strategy audit instrument in the corpus.
It is assembled from the evidence below and must be presented as a structured judgment, not a
published scoring model.

---

## The lens

A brand strategy is only real if it constrains decisions. Every row below tests one question:
**would this line change what the business does?** A statement that survives any decision is
decoration regardless of how well written it is.

Two evidenced additions shape the scoring:

- **Distinctiveness is scored separately from differentiation.** They are different claims with
  different evidence `[C]` [s1] [s2] [s7] versus `[C]` [s62] [s63], and a brand can be
  meaningfully unique yet unrecognisable, or instantly recognisable with no reason to choose it.
  Collapsing them hides the more common failure, which is the first.
- **Identity and image are different objects** `[C]` [s53] [s54]. What the company projects and
  what customers perceive are separate, and the audit must not assume the deck describes
  reality. Where possible, check the projection against the site, the reviews, and the sales
  material.

---

## The scorecard

Seven rows. Each is **Strong / Workable / Weak / Missing**. Lead the deliverable with this
table, then justify every row beneath it.

| # | Dimension | Strong means |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Position** | Names a frame of reference, a target, and a point of difference someone could dispute. Not a category description |
| 2 | **Distinctiveness** | Has ownable assets people could actually recognise it by `[C]` [s7]. Not just a claim of uniqueness |
| 3 | **Personality** | Traits with behavioural definitions a writer and designer can both act on. Not three positive adjectives |
| 4 | **Promise** | Specific enough that failing it would be noticed, and the business has a mechanism to keep it |
| 5 | **Story** | A real narrative with a reason the business exists. Not a company timeline |
| 6 | **Values** | Each carries a trade-off. Constrains a hiring or pricing decision |
| 7 | **Exclusions** | Names customers, work, and positions it is deliberately giving up |

**Scoring discipline.** Weak and Missing are different: Weak means present but not
load-bearing, Missing means absent. Do not soften. A scorecard where everything is Workable
tells the client nothing and is the most common way this deliverable fails.

**Calibration.** Most real brand strategies score Weak or Missing on rows 1, 2 and 7. The three
dominant failure patterns are: a position that is really a category description, no distinctive
assets at all, and nothing ruled out. If you score a document Strong across the board, re-read
it looking for the choice it is avoiding.

---

## Reading order

1. **Cover the logo and read the position aloud.** Would it fit a competitor? If yes, row 1 is
   Weak at best regardless of how it is written.
2. **List what a customer could recognise them by** without the name present. Colour, shape,
   character, phrase, sound, layout. An empty list is row 2 Missing, and for a small brand that
   is usually the most consequential finding in the audit.
3. **Test each personality trait for its opposite.** If the opposite is obviously undesirable,
   the trait carries no information. "Trustworthy" fails. "Blunt" passes.
4. **Ask how the promise fails.** If they cannot describe breaking it, it is not operational.
5. **Check the story is true and is a story.** Founded, grew, opened an office is a timeline.
   Also check it is *theirs*, invented origins are the highest-risk content in a brand doc.
6. **Check each value costs something.** If holding it never loses them a client or a hire, cut
   it.
7. **Look for the exclusions.** Usually absent. Their absence is the finding.
8. **Compare projection against reality.** Read the deck, then the homepage, then the reviews.
   Identity-image gaps `[C]` [s53] are where the real work usually is.

---

## Then fix what is worth fixing

Rank the gaps by leverage, not by how broken they are. Rewrite the two or three highest-leverage
pieces in full so the client can see the standard, rather than describing what a better version
would contain.

Row 1 and row 7 are usually the highest leverage, because everything else inherits from them. A
personality section cannot be fixed while the position underneath it is a category description.

**If the document turns out not to be a brand strategy at all**: it is a marketing plan, a
services list, or a mission statement with a colour palette stapled on, say so and offer to
switch to build mode. Auditing a document for failing to be something it never was is not
useful to anyone.

**If the real problem is upstream**, in the business position, the offer, or the target
customer, route to `strategic-foundation`. A brand strategy cannot fix an unsettled business.
