# Platform formatting — how to format a post by platform and by topic

Load this when the ask is "what should this actually look like on LinkedIn / Instagram /
Meta ads / email". It answers two questions that interact: **what the platform does to your
text**, and **what the topic requires**.

Tiers: `[C]` confirmed, `[P]` practitioner, `[K]` craft. Evidence in
`copywriting-advisor/references/research-synthesis.md`.

---

## The standing rule, before any number below

**Verify every character limit against the platform's own documentation on the day you write,
and record the date in the deliverable.**

This is not caution for its own sake. The corpus contains the official help centres for Meta,
Google Ads and LinkedIn `[P]` [s418], and the q29 report found that **Google's own
responsive-search-ad page does not state character counts at all** - the widely quoted 30/90
figures come from third-party counter tools `[P]` [s419][s420]. A number that everyone
repeats and no official page states is the exact pattern this skill exists to catch.

**Truncation is not the limit.** The limit is what the field accepts; truncation is what the
reader sees before "... more" on their device. Truncation is the one that decides whether your
first line works, and it differs by placement and by device.

## What the engagement research actually shows

Added 2026-08-15 when a corrected research pass recovered the empirical layer this file
originally lacked. **These are the findings to reason from; character counts are trivia by
comparison.**

| Platform | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Facebook** | 106,316 messages across 782 companies, content-coded, relating advertising content to user engagement `[C]` [s432]. Separately, readability, text length and hashtag count in branded image posts against engagement and brand awareness `[C]` [s430] |
| **Instagram** | 21,692 ads analysed on psycholinguistic dimensions against click-through rate from Meta's Ads Manager `[C]` [s431]. Mega-influencer caption language and punctuation `[C]` [s328] |
| **Email** | Personalising the subject line with the recipient's name lifts opens ~20% in randomized field experiments across millions of emails `[C]` [s441][s426], with a published replication testing whether it still holds `[C]` [s427]. Linguistic structure of 31,812 subject lines against open rate `[C]` [s423] |
| **Cross-platform** | Whether AI-generated content performs comparably to human-written across platforms `[C]` [s458] |

**"Shorter is better" is an assumption, not a finding.** It has been tested directly across
multiple outlets `[C]` [s457]. Do not assert brevity wins as settled.

## What actually generalises across platforms

Only three things, and they are the ones worth spending effort on.

1. **Front-load the point.** Attention is front-loaded rather than absent: above the fold takes
   57% of viewing time, the second screenful 17% `[C]` [s259]. On a feed the equivalent is the
   pre-truncation line.
2. **Write simply.** Simpler wording outperformed complex across 30,000+ field experiments
   `[C]` [s76], and needless complexity lowers judged intelligence `[C]` [s136]. This holds
   everywhere and costs nothing.
3. **Match the promise to the destination.** Craft sources converge on ad-to-landing-page
   alignment as a primary failure point `[K]` [s385][s396]. A post that over-promises relative
   to where it sends people underperforms twice.

Almost everything else is platform-specific convention, and convention is `[K]` at best.

## By platform

### LinkedIn (organic)

| Aspect | Guidance |
|---|---|
| First line | The whole game. It is what shows before "see more" - put the claim or tension there, never a preamble or a greeting |
| Structure | Short paragraphs, generous line breaks. Dense blocks are skipped rather than read |
| Length | Long is fine when the substance justifies it; length should follow what the reader needs to decide `[C]` [s24][s1] |
| Links | Convention holds that in-post links suppress reach. **Unverified** - no source in this corpus tests it. Treat as a hypothesis, not a rule |
| Ads | Sponsored content splits into intro text, headline, description; B2B guidance is benefits-led with explicit credibility signals `[K]` [s395] |

### Instagram

| Aspect | Guidance |
|---|---|
| First line | Truncates early. The hook must survive one short line |
| Structure | Caption carries the argument; the image carries the stop. They are one unit, never written separately |
| Hashtags | Convention rather than evidence. No source here measures them |
| Format split | Feed carousel, reel caption and story text are different jobs. Do not reuse one text across all three |

### Meta ads (Facebook / Instagram)

Field structure matters more than any single count: **primary text**, **headline**,
**description**, each behaving differently by placement `[K]` [s392].

Vendor-reported recommendations, **not** the official spec page `[P]` [s419]:

| Field | Reported recommendation | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Primary text | ~125 characters | Truncates by placement |
| Headline | ~40 characters | Facebook Feed specifically ~27 to avoid truncation |
| Description | ~25 characters | Frequently not shown at all |

Craft sources use hook / body / CTA structure and name PAS, AIDA, social proof and curiosity
gap as the arranging frames `[K]` [s391][s405]. **Those are scaffolds, not evidence** - no
source in this corpus tests one against another.

### Google Ads (responsive search)

Widely quoted as 30-character headlines and 90-character descriptions, from counter tools
rather than Google's page `[P]` [s419][s420]. Verify.

Craft guidance `[K]` [s385][s396][s399]: fill the asset slots with genuinely varied,
non-redundant copy rather than near-duplicates; expect the system to test combinations, so
each asset must stand alone; track conversion outcomes alongside Ad Strength rather than
optimising the score itself.

### Email

The weakest evidence area in the whole corpus, and the skill says so. Every email benchmark
here is vendor-published `[P]`.

| Aspect | Guidance |
|---|---|
| Subject line | Front-load. Mobile truncation is the real constraint and varies by client |
| Preview text | A second line of copy, not a repeat of the subject. Most senders waste it |
| Body | One purpose, one CTA per email. A sequence where every email makes the same ask in different words is one email sent five times |
| **Open rate** | **Do not optimise against it.** Apple MPP has inflated it since 2021; use clicks |

### X

Thinnest coverage here. No confirmed source. Front-loading and simplicity carry over; specific
conventions should be verified live rather than taken from this file.

## By topic

Platform sets the container. **Topic sets the shape.** The same announcement is not the same
post as a teaching piece, even on the same platform.

| Topic / intent | Shape that fits | Fails when |
|---|---|---|
| **Teaching / how-to** | Claim first, then steps. Numbered or clearly delimited so it survives skimming | It buries the lesson under a story nobody asked for |
| **Case study / result** | Situation, what was actually done, outcome with a real number, what transfers | The number is vague, or the client is unnamed with nothing concrete in its place |
| **Opinion / contrarian** | State the position plainly, name the received view you reject, give the condition under which you would be wrong | It is contrarian without an argument, which reads as bait |
| **Announcement** | What changed, who it affects, what to do. Short | It is written as excitement rather than information |
| **Promotion / offer** | One action, the proof, the risk reversal. Follows the conversion rules, not the content rules | It hides the ask, or stacks three CTAs |
| **Story / personal** | A specific moment, not a general reflection. Concreteness raises interest and comprehension `[C]` [s134] | The lesson is generic enough to fit any story |

**The intersection rule.** When platform convention and topic requirement conflict, topic
wins on structure and platform wins on formatting. A case study on LinkedIn still needs its
number in the first line, because that is what survives truncation - but it does not become a
listicle just because listicles do well there.

## What not to do

- **Do not reuse one text across platforms.** Repurposing is fine; identical posting is not,
  because the truncation point and field structure differ.
- **Do not quote a character limit from memory or from this file.** Verify and date it.
- **Do not treat an engagement convention as a finding.** Link suppression, hashtag counts,
  emoji use and posting times are conventions with no confirmed source in this corpus. Say
  so when a client asks.
- **Do not let format substitute for substance.** No amount of line-breaking fixes a post with
  nothing in it, and the fold research says the top of the message is what gets read `[C]`
  [s259] - which is an argument about the first sentence, not about whitespace.
