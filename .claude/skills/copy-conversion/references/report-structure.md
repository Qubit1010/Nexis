# Report structure — `17-conversion-copy.md`

Section order for the deliverable. Each section carries a **fails when** test: if that
sentence is true of what you wrote, the section is not done.

Client-facing document. **Never mention NexusPoint or Aleem in it.**

---

## 0. What we know

A fact table before any copy, same pattern as `07-strategic-foundation.md`.

| Fact | Source | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| ... | their site / the brief / `13` / inference | `[verified]` / `[research]` / `[inferred]` / `[assumption]` |

Include the **proof inventory** here: every number, named customer, guarantee,
certification and result the client actually has. This is the section the rest of the
document is constrained by.

**Fails when** the copy later claims something this table does not support.

---

## 1. The brief, restated

One short section: who this copy talks to, what they believe on arrival, the single action,
and the one thing they must believe to take it. Restated in our words so a misunderstanding
surfaces here rather than in the copy.

**Fails when** it could describe any client in the category.

---

## 2. The copy

The bulk of the document. One subsection per asset. Every asset carries, in this order:

1. **Purpose line**: the one action, and where the reader came from.
2. **The copy itself**, complete and formatted as it will appear. Not a description of it.
3. **Rationale**, three lines maximum: the structural frame used, the customer language
   drawn on, the proof carrying the claim. Trace each voice choice to a line in `14`.
4. **Variants**, only where a genuine strategic alternative exists, each labelled with the
   angle it takes rather than "Option B".

**Fails when** the client would have to rewrite it before using it, or when a rationale line
says "this is more engaging" without naming what makes it so.

### Per-asset structure

| Asset | Must contain | Fails when |
|---|---|---|
| **Landing page** | Hero (headline, subhead, CTA), the problem framed in the reader's words, the offer, proof, objection handling, close. Navigation decision stated | It reads as a homepage - trying to serve every visitor rather than the one who clicked one ad |
| **Homepage** | Who it is for and what it does, above the fold, in the customer's vocabulary. Paths for the distinct audiences that genuinely exist | A visitor cannot say what the company does after five seconds |
| **Sales page** | Long-form argument: problem, mechanism, offer, proof, objections in the order they actually arise, risk reversal, close, FAQ | Objections are answered in the order convenient to the argument rather than the order the reader raises them |
| **Email / sequence** | Per email: purpose, subject line options, preview text, body, one CTA. Sequence-level: what each email is for and why it is spaced that way | Every email in the sequence makes the same ask in different words |
| **Ad copy** | Per platform and placement: the hook, the body, the CTA, and the landing page it matches | The ad promises something the landing page does not immediately confirm |
| **Product description** | What it is, who it is for, what it does for them, the specifications a buyer actually compares on | It is all atmosphere and a buyer cannot compare it against an alternative |
| **CTA / microcopy** | The button text, surrounding reassurance, and what happens next | The button says "Submit", or the reader cannot tell what happens after the click |
| **Popup / lead magnet** | Trigger condition, the offer, the exchange being asked for | The value offered is worth less than the email address requested |
| **Social post** | The pre-truncation first line, the body shaped to the topic, one action. Per platform, per `platform-formatting.md` | The same text is pasted across platforms, or the hook does not survive the "see more" cut |

## 3. Platform format constraints

A table of the constraints each asset is written against.

**Verify limits against the platform's own documentation at the time of writing, and say in
the document that you did.** Ad and social platforms change character limits and truncation
points without notice, so a number hardcoded into a skill file is wrong within a year. Where
a limit could not be verified, write `unverified` rather than a remembered number.

Start from the primary source in the corpus - Google's own responsive-search-ad documentation
`[K]` [s418] - then the practitioner cheat sheets `[K]` [s419][s420] only as a pointer to
what to go and check. **A cheat sheet is never the citation**; the platform's live docs are.

For the shape of the copy inside those limits (Meta primary text versus headline versus
description, LinkedIn intro text, responsive search asset strategy), the craft tier has
worked examples: `[K]` [s385][s392][s391][s405][s395][s396]. Use them for structure, never
as evidence that a structure performs better.

Truncation is the constraint that actually matters and it is not the same as the limit: the
question is what a reader sees before "... more" on the device they are using, not what the
field accepts.

**Fails when** a character count appears with no source and no verification date.

## 6. What to measure

What to watch, at what volume it would mean anything, and what it will not tell you.

State plainly that a single test on ordinary traffic will not resolve small differences.
See the measurement warning in `review-rubric.md`.

**Fails when** it promises a lift figure. We do not know it, and the client will hold us to it.
