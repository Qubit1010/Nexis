# Review rubric — auditing existing conversion copy

Load this in audit mode. Score, then **rewrite the highest-leverage pieces**. An audit that
describes what is wrong without showing what right looks like is a list of complaints.

Grades: **Strong** / **Workable** / **Weak** / **Missing**.

**Calibration.** Most real client copy scores Weak or Missing on rows 2, 3 and 7. That is the
expected result, not a harsh one, and the scorecard is worthless if everything comes back
Workable to avoid an awkward conversation. Say the true grade and show the fix.

---

## The scorecard

| # | Row | Strong | Weak / Missing |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Single action** | One named action per asset; everything on the page serves it | Competing CTAs of equal weight; "learn more" alongside "buy now" alongside a newsletter signup |
| 2 | **Specificity** | Claims carry numbers, timeframes, named things | "Save time", "boost growth", "world-class", "innovative solutions" |
| 3 | **Proof** | Every load-bearing claim has a fact, number, named customer or demonstration behind it | Claims float unsupported; testimonials are anonymous or say nothing; logos with no story |
| 4 | **Customer language** | Uses the words customers use, traceable to reviews, calls or `08` | Internal vocabulary, category jargon, or language invented in a workshop |
| 5 | **Objections** | The real hesitations are named and answered where they arise | Objections ignored, or answered only in an FAQ at the bottom where the hesitant reader never reaches |
| 6 | **Voice fidelity** | Consistent with `14-brand-voice.md`; a reader would recognise it as the same company across assets | Each page sounds like whoever wrote it; violates the never-use list |
| 7 | **Honest claims** | Everything is substantiable; testimonials meet FTC requirements; no manufactured scarcity | Unverifiable superlatives, fake countdown timers, invented statistics, testimonials without the disclosures the law requires |

Row 7 is the one that carries actual liability. Grade it strictly, and treat a failure there
as urgent rather than as a style note.

---

## The five-second test

Before scoring anything: read the hero, then look away and answer aloud: what does this
company do, who is it for, and what am I meant to do next?

If any of the three is unanswerable, rows 1 to 4 cannot score above Weak regardless of how
the rest of the page reads. Everything below the fold is written for someone who already got
past this.

---

## What to rewrite

Rank fixes by leverage, not by how wrong they are. In practice:

1. **The hero**: most-read, and the cheapest thing to change.
2. **The primary CTA and the copy immediately around it**: everything upstream exists to
   get the reader here.
3. **The single weakest unsupported claim**: the one a sceptical reader would stop on.

Rewrite those three properly, in the client's voice, with the rationale traced to a rule.
Then list the rest as findings. Rewriting everything at audit stage is how an audit turns
into an unbilled rebuild.

---

## The measurement warning

State this in the deliverable, every time, in plain words.

Copy changes are not reliably measurable at the traffic volumes most SMB clients have. A test
needs enough conversions per variant to distinguish a real difference from noise, and small
differences need far more traffic than clients expect. A test that runs until it looks like a
win and is then stopped has selected for noise, which is why so many reported wins do not
survive.

So: recommend changes on the argument for them - clarity, proof, matching what the customer
actually asks - and be honest that attribution will usually be directional rather than
conclusive. **Never promise a percentage lift.** The number would be invented, and the client
will hold us to it.

Where a change is genuinely testable (high-traffic page, large expected difference), say so
and say what would need to be true for the test to mean anything.
