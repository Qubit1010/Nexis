# Instagram

**Date warning specific to this platform.** Instagram's most-cited explainer, Mosseri's
"Shedding More Light on How Instagram Works" `[P*]` [s286], is dated **8 June 2021 with a 31 May

## 1. What it is for

Visual and consumer-facing brands, local businesses, creators, and any audience reachable
through recommendation rather than through an existing following. Its distinguishing property
is that **reach is not gated on follower count** to the degree it is elsewhere.

Not for: complex B2B sales, text-first explanation, anything needing a link in the post.

## 2. How it ranks — `[P*]`

**Surface by surface, not one algorithm.** Feed, Reels, Stories, Explore and Search rank
separately with different signals; Instagram documents Feed on its own `[P*]` [s312]. Answering
"the Instagram algorithm" as one system is the most common error here.

**Recommendation eligibility is a separate gate from ranking.** Instagram documents an account
and content eligibility layer determining whether you can be recommended to **non-followers** at
all, in Explore, Reels and Feed Recommendations, and separately whether the account can surface
in Search and Suggested `[P*]` [s311]. Account Status exposes this to the creator.

**This is the practical answer to most "am I shadowbanned" questions on Instagram**: there is a
documented, checkable, first-party eligibility state. Send them to Account Status before
theorising.

Harmful-content handling is documented separately `[P*]` [s317].

## 3. What changed — dated

**30 April 2024, and now fully launched** `[P*]` [s314][s315]. Instagram stated plainly that its
ranking had favoured large accounts:

> "Historically because of how we've ranked content, creators with large followings and
> aggregators that share copies of original content have gotten more reach in recommendations
> than smaller, original content creators. We think it's important to correct this to give all
> creators a more equal chance of breaking through to new audiences." `[P*]` [s314]

The stated correction includes **a new ranking input favouring smaller creators** and measures
against aggregators reposting others' work. Instagram later confirmed the reels-ranking changes
"are now fully launched" `[P*]` [s315].

Two consequences worth stating to a client:
- A small account is **not** structurally locked out here the way it is on LinkedIn.
- **Originality is a ranking input.** Reposting others' content is a named target. This is the
  same direction Facebook and LinkedIn moved in 2026 (see `platform-scoreboard.md`).

Instagram's own reach guidance: `[P*]` [s287][s294][s297].

## 4. Formats that win

Reels reach non-followers; feed posts and carousels serve the existing audience. This is
directional and consistent with the recommendation surfaces being separate `[P*]` [s311][s312].

**The specific numbers in circulation are practitioner-tier**, including the sends-and-saves
weightings in `marketing-advisor`. There is no confirmed weighting of saves or sends against
likes anywhere in this corpus, and the engagement literature explicitly warns that studies
aggregate these behaviours rather than separating them `[C]` [s1]. Give direction, attribute
numbers.

## 6. Growth mechanics

- **Recommendation, not follower accumulation, is the growth engine.** Eligibility for
  non-follower surfaces `[P*]` [s311] is the thing to fix first, and it is checkable.
- **Originality is now a ranking input** `[P*]` [s314], so a repost-led strategy is working
  against the ranker.
- **Smaller accounts have a stated ranking allowance** `[P*]` [s314], which is the opposite of
  the "you need N followers" folklore. See `what-not-to-do.md`.
- Comment-to-DM capture is a real mechanic; magnitudes are practitioner-tier.

## 7. Fails when

- The offer needs a clickable link in-post.
- The content is reposted or aggregated. Directly targeted `[P*]` [s314].
- The buying decision is long and committee-based.
- The client cannot sustain video production.
