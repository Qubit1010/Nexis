## 1. What it is for

Local and community businesses, older demographics, Groups, and events. **Groups are usually the
real answer on this platform**, not the Page.

Not for: B2B, professional services sold to decision-makers at work, or any brand whose plan is
"post on the Page and get reach".

## 2. How it ranks — `[P*]`

**Four steps.** Meta documents the process as four steps answering "What content is likely to
matter most to your readers?", covering "what content we can show, what we know about the post,
and how relevant it will be to people" `[P*]` [s191].

**Connected versus recommended content.** Meta describes Feed as a mix of "connected content
(e.g., content from the people you're friends with or are following, Groups you've joined, and
Pages you've liked)" and "recommended content (e.g., content we think you'll be interested in)"
`[P*]` [s192].

That distinction is the strategically important one. A Page post competes in the **connected**
lane against friends and Groups, and in the **recommended** lane against everything. Meta also
publishes a general ranking-explanation approach `[P*]` [s203], a Widely Viewed Content Report
covering what people actually see `[P*]` [s194], and the Pages API `[P*]` [s195].

## 3. What changed — dated

**March 2026: originality rewarded, unoriginal content deprioritised.** `[P*]` [s193]

> "We're sharing our progress on rewarding creators who post original content on Facebook with
> greater reach and monetization, while deprioritizing unoriginal content."

Meta also states it "updated our original content guidelines to give creators more clarity on
how to get their work seen and recommended in Feed and Reels", and is testing impersonation
detection tooling `[P*]` [s193].

**This is the same move Instagram made in 2024 and LinkedIn announced in March 2026.** Three
platforms, three first-party statements, one direction. See `platform-scoreboard.md`.

## 4. Formats that win

Reels reach the recommended lane; Page text posts largely do not. Groups content reaches the
connected lane. Directional, from the connected-versus-recommended split `[P*]` [s192]; the
specific numbers in circulation are practitioner-tier.

No first-party statement. Convention only.

## 6. Growth mechanics

- **Choose the lane deliberately.** Growing a Page means competing in recommendations; a Group
  means owning connected distribution. They are different strategies `[P*]` [s192].
- **Originality is now a stated reach and monetisation input** `[P*]` [s193].
- Check the Widely Viewed Content Report `[P*]` [s194] before accepting anyone's claim about
  what "does well on Facebook". It is Meta's own account of what people actually see.

## 7. Fails when

- The audience is B2B. Route to LinkedIn.
- The plan is Page-posting for organic reach. This is the single most common wasted retainer
  line-item on this platform.
- The content is repurposed from elsewhere without adaptation. Named target `[P*]` [s193].

**Be willing to recommend against a Facebook Page entirely.** For many clients the honest
answer is a Group, or nothing.
