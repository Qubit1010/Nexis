## The eight questions

1. **What it is for**: who this platform actually suits, and who it does not
2. **How it ranks**: `[P*]` first-party statements, quoted with a retrieval date
3. **What changed**: recent platform changes, dated
4. **Formats that win**: and the evidence tier behind each claim
5. **Cadence**: what is documented versus what is convention
6. **Growth mechanics**: how an account actually gains reach here
7. **Fails when**: the conditions under which this platform is the wrong answer
8. **Who executes**: the skill that makes the thing

## Three standing rules

**Every `[P*]` claim is what the platform SAYS, not what is true.** A platform describing its
own ranking has a commercial interest and publishes no method. It is authoritative for what it
requires or defines, and it is never evidence that a tactic works. Always attach the retrieval
date; every one of these pages is revised without notice.

**Never extrapolate between platforms.** A confirmed finding about one recommender does not
transfer to another. Threads is not Instagram, whatever the shared ownership.

**The corpus is unevenly deep and the table above says how.** With 17 first-party documents,
LinkedIn can be answered in detail. Reddit has **zero**, X has one and it is a media-literacy
policy rather than anything about ranking. Say the coverage is thin rather than reaching for a
neighbouring platform.
