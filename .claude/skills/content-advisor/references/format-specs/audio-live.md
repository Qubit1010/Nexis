# Audio and live — podcasts, webinars

Two formats sharing a mechanic nothing else in the 19 has: **a standing time commitment from
both sides.** A podcast is a recurring obligation; a webinar asks an audience to be somewhere at
a fixed hour. That commitment is the reason both formats build unusual trust, and the reason
both fail more often on capacity than on content.

**Be honest about the evidence here.** This is the thinnest-supported pair in the corpus. Most
of what circulates as podcast and webinar best practice is `[K]` or vendor-published `[P]`, and
the spec files say so rather than dressing convention as finding.

---

## Podcasts

**What it is for.** Depth and relationship. The host-listener connection is the mechanism, and
it is the one thing here with peer-reviewed support: listening motivations and outcomes are
researched `[C]` [s36].

**What the evidence does NOT cover.** Audience sizes, video-podcast preference shares and format
splits all came back vendor-published `[P]`. Treat every podcast market number as a directional
vendor claim and label it. See `research-synthesis.md` weakness 3.

**Structure.** Cold open on the strongest moment. Intro that states what this episode gives.
Body in segments. Payoff. One action. `[K]` [s291][s308][s353]

**The hook.** The first 60 seconds decide whether a new listener stays. Craft consensus is to
open on a moment from the middle of the conversation rather than on introductions. `[K]`
[s299][s317]

**Length and pacing.** Set by the conversation, not by a target. The six-minute video finding
does not transfer - audio is consumed while doing something else, which is the whole point of
the format.

**2026 optimization.** Video-capable recording for discovery clips. Real timestamps from the
transcript, never estimated. Show notes that stand alone. `[K]` [s300][s348][s360]

**Distribution.** The feed is the smallest part. Clips carry discovery, show notes carry search,
and the back catalogue is an asset most clients forget they own.

**Fails when** it is launched as a project rather than a commitment. A podcast that stops after
six episodes costs more credibility than never starting.

**Who executes it.** `content-production` for structure and show notes. `podcast-repurposer` for
turning a transcript into short-form pieces.

## Webinars

**There is no webinar literature.** No confirmed source in this corpus studies webinars as
practised. What exists is adjacent and worth using carefully:

- **Live streaming commerce**, where real-time interaction measurably changes sustained
  engagement against a simulated condition `[C]` [s10], and social presence is positively
  associated with purchase intention across several SEM studies `[C]` [s51][s56][s42][s27].
- **The review of that field** notes heterogeneity across settings, dominance of survey and
  PLS-SEM designs, and explicitly calls for more experiments `[C]` [s17].
- **Synchronous versus asynchronous instruction** and its completion effects.

**Everything specific to a B2B webinar is `[K]`.** Say so when advising.

**What it is for.** Depth plus interaction, with a registration list as a by-product. The list is
often worth more than the session.

**Structure.** Pre-frame in the first two minutes: what they leave with. Three blocks maximum.
An interaction point per block. A plan for the drop-off moment. Q&A seeded with three real
questions. The offer once, at the end if there is one. `[K]` [s287][s318][s324]

**The hook.** The first two minutes, and they are competing with the attendee's inbox. Open on
the outcome, not on housekeeping or speaker bios.

**Length and pacing.** Attrition over a session is real - it is the one thing the live-commerce
evidence directly supports `[C]` [s10]. Front-load the value rather than saving it for the end.

**2026 optimization.** Registration and reminder sequences do more for attendance than anything
in the session itself `[K]` [s293][s331]. Plan the recording's repurposing before recording, not
after - it is usually the highest-yield asset in a content operation and usually the most
neglected.

**Distribution.** Registration sequence, live, then the recording as an on-demand asset plus
derivatives.

**Fails when** it is a slide deck read aloud, when the offer arrives before the value, or when
nobody planned what happens to the recording.

**Who executes it.** `content-production`.

## What generalises across both

1. **Capacity is the constraint, not creativity.** Both formats are recurring commitments, and
   both die from the same cause: a cadence the team could not hold. Size before starting.
2. **The derivative assets are usually worth more than the original.** A one-hour recording that
   nobody clips is a one-hour recording nobody watches.
3. **Real timestamps, never estimated.** For both show notes and clip selection.
4. **The mechanism is relationship**, which is slow. Neither format shows results on the
   timescale a client expects, and saying that upfront is part of the deliverable.
