# Newsletter

One format, and the only one in the 19 with a mechanic nobody else has: **you own the
distribution list.** No feed ranks it, no algorithm gates it, and nobody can take the audience
away. That is the whole strategic case for the format, and it is why a newsletter is worth more
than its engagement numbers suggest.

Tiers per `00-index.md`.

**Boundary:** subject lines, preview text and any copy whose job is to make someone act belong
to `copywriting-advisor` and `copy-conversion`, which hold 21 confirmed sources on email field
experiments including the personalisation work. **This file covers the newsletter as a content
asset and an owned channel. Cross-cite the copy layer, do not restate it.**

---

## What it is for

A recurring, owned relationship with an audience that opted in. It compounds, which almost
nothing else here does, and it is the only format where reach does not decay with platform
policy.

---

## Structure

Subject line and preview text (that layer is `copy-conversion`'s). Then: an opening that gives
one idea immediately, the body in whichever of the two shapes fits, the takeaway, one CTA, and
a sign-off. `[K]` [s305][s321][s339]

**Two shapes, and mixing them is the most common structural failure:**

| | Single-topic | Roundup |
|---|---|---|
| Job | Make one argument | Curate and contextualise |
| Reader gets | A position | A filter |
| Fails when | It becomes a blog post in an inbox | The curation adds nothing the reader could not get from the links |

Pick one per issue. An issue that opens with an argument and then appends five links is neither.

---

## The hook

The first line of the body, not the subject line. The subject earns the open; the first line
earns the read, and they have different jobs. Craft consensus is to open on the idea rather than
on housekeeping, greetings or "in this issue". `[K]` [s296][s314][s329]

---

## Length and pacing

Set by the shape and by the cadence the client can hold. **Cadence is the constraint, not
length** - a weekly newsletter that becomes fortnightly by accident is a broken promise the
subscriber notices.

The frequency evidence is genuinely unhelpful as a universal number: effective frequency has
been meta-analysed `[C]` [s99] and carryover effects likewise `[C]` [s108], and the formal
scheduling work concludes the optimum depends on carryover and response curvature rather than
prescribing a figure `[C]`. **Answer "how often" with capacity.**

---

## 2026 optimization

**The open rate is broken, and this is the single most important thing to tell a client about
this format.** Apple Mail Privacy Protection preloads remote images through a proxy, registering
"opens" the recipient never performed, and obscures IP, location and open time `[P]`.

Consequences that must be stated in any measurement section:

- **Pre-2021 and post-2021 open rates are not comparable.**
- **Two lists with different Apple Mail shares are not comparable to each other.**
- No peer-reviewed quantification of the inflation exists in this corpus. The widely repeated
  "15-20%" is a practitioner opinion post `[P]`.
- **Optimise on clicks and downstream action.** Not on opens, and never on an open-rate
  benchmark from a vendor table.

**List growth and attrition are the real health metrics.** Unsubscribes concentrate around
frequency changes and content drift, which is a cadence problem rather than a copy problem.

---

## Distribution

The list is the distribution - but the list itself has to be built. Growth comes from the rest
of the content operation: a gated asset, a site placement, the podcast, the social surfaces.
**A newsletter with no acquisition path attached is a format with no audience**, and it is the
most common way this one fails quietly.

Archive issues publicly where it suits the client. It gives the format a search surface it
otherwise lacks.

---

## Fails when

- It is a company update nobody subscribed for.
- Cadence was set by ambition rather than capacity, so it drifts and then stops.
- It mixes the two shapes, so it is neither an argument nor a filter.
- Performance is judged on open rate.
- There is no acquisition path, so the list never compounds.

## LinkedIn newsletters — a different thing

A LinkedIn newsletter is **not** an owned channel. It is a platform-hosted subscription that the
platform ranks, gates and can change. It shares the format's structure and none of its strategic
case: you do not own the list and cannot export the relationship.

Advise it as a distribution surface, never as a substitute for an owned list. Specifications
are `[P*]` and must be verified live.
