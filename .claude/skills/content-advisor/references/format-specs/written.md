# Written long-form — blog posts, articles, guides, case studies, whitepapers, ebooks

Six formats sharing one mechanic: **the reader is in control of pace and can leave at any
line.** Nothing autoplays. That is what separates this group from video and what makes the
opening and the scannable structure load-bearing rather than stylistic.

Tiers per `00-index.md`. `[K]` informs structure and technique; it can never support a claim.

**On-page search mechanics are `blog-writer`'s and `seo-onpage`'s**, on their own corpora. This
file does not restate titles, metas, internal linking or answer-block thresholds.

---

## Blog posts

**What it is for.** Recurring, indexed, search-and-share visible. The workhorse that
accumulates.

**Structure.** Answer-first: the question, then the answer, then the support. Sub-headed so it
survives scanning.

**The hook.** The first paragraph states the answer or the tension. Not context, not a
definition of the topic, not "in today's fast-moving landscape".

**Length and pacing.** Set by the question. Do not pad to a word count and do not truncate on
principle - "shorter is better" is an assumption that has been tested against directly, not a
finding.

**2026 optimization.** Extractable blocks, comparison tables, an FAQ, and structure that answers
the follow-up questions a reader would ask next. Owned by `blog-writer`, which carries an
83-source AEO/GEO corpus. Cross-cite it.

**Distribution.** Search plus one owned channel on day one. A blog post with no distribution row
is root cause 1 in `diagnosis-playbooks.md`.

**Fails when** it answers a question the audience never asked, or when it passes the swap test -
a competitor could publish it unchanged.

**Who executes it.** `blog-writer`.

## Articles

Same mechanics as a blog post, different placement: a publication, a LinkedIn article, a
partner site. **The difference that matters is the audience is not yours** - assume no context
and no prior trust, and lead harder.

## Guides

**What it is for.** The definitive treatment of one problem, meant to be the last thing someone
needs to read on it.

**Two versions, and they are genuinely different documents:**

| | On-site pillar | Gated download |
|---|---|---|
| Job | Rank, be cited, accumulate | Trade substance for a contact |
| Opening | Answer-first | Promise-first: what they can *do* after reading |
| Structure | Sub-problems as sections, linked to cluster pages | Sub-problems as chapters, each ending in an action |
| The kept part | Internal links | A template, checklist or calculator |
| Owner | `blog-writer` | `content-production` |

**Gating is a real trade, not a free win.** It costs reach to buy a contact. If the substance is
thin, the download disappoints and the address is worth less than the goodwill it cost. Say so
rather than gating anyway. `[K]` [s366][s377][s389]

**Fails when** it is a long blog post with a form in front of it.

## Case studies

**What it is for.** Proof, in a form a prospect can map onto themselves.

**Structure.** Result in the headline. Then: who the customer is and why they are recognisable,
the situation before with its cost, what was tried including what did not work, what was done,
the result with its measurement method and timeframe, what made it work, and a real quote.
`[K]` [s304][s372][s381]

**The hook.** The specific result, in the headline. "How X cut onboarding from 6 weeks to 9
days", not "A success story with X".

**Length and pacing.** Long enough to be credible, short enough to be read before a call.

**2026 optimization.** The transferable mechanism is the part that gets shared and cited - the
part that says *why* it worked. A case study that only reports an outcome is a testimonial.

**Distribution.** Sales enablement first, site second, social third. This is one of the few
formats where the internal audience matters as much as the external one.

**Fails when** the numbers are invented, unattributed or unmeasurable. **Never invent a
customer name, quote or result.** If the client will not disclose figures, report the
qualitative change and label it - a case study with a fabricated number is worse than one with
none.

**Who executes it.** `content-production` for a client's customer. `case-study-generator` for
NexusPoint's own projects.

## Whitepapers

**What it is for.** To carry one argument that needs evidence, to an audience that will check.

**Structure.** Title states the finding, not the topic. Executive summary that stands alone.
The problem in the reader's terms with its cost. What is currently done and where it breaks. The
evidence - the section the document exists for. What follows from it. **Limitations.** What to
do. Method and sources. `[K]` [s390][s393][s397]

**The hook.** The finding, in the title and again in the first line of the summary.

**Length and pacing.** As long as the argument needs and no longer. Write the executive summary
last; if it cannot stand alone the argument is not finished.

**2026 optimization.** The limitations section is what makes the rest credible, and it is the
one most often cut. Keep it.

**Distribution.** Usually gated. Sales-led, plus an ungated summary that does the reach work.

**Fails when** the central claim rests on a number the client cannot substantiate. Inventory the
proof before drafting - it decides what the document is allowed to argue.

**Who executes it.** `content-production`.

## Ebooks

**What it is for.** Breadth rather than depth. A whitepaper argues one thing; an ebook covers a
territory.

**Structure.** Cover promise as one specific outcome. Who it is for and who it is not for - the
exclusion is what earns trust. A chapter per sub-problem, each ending in something the reader
can do. One worked example carried all the way through. A checklist or template. One next step.
`[K]` [s402][s404][s409]

**Length and pacing.** Chapters short enough to finish in a sitting. Nobody reads an ebook
straight through, so each chapter has to survive being read alone.

**Fails when** it is padded to look substantial. Length signals effort to the producer and cost
to the reader.

**Who executes it.** `content-production`.

## What generalises across all six

1. **The reader can leave at any line**, so every section earns the next one.
2. **Structure for scanning**, because that is how these are actually read.
3. **Graphics carry what must be remembered** - they beat text for long-term retention `[C]`
   [s34] and improve reading comprehension by meta-analysis `[C]` [s239]. Use them for the
   load-bearing idea, not for decoration.
4. **Proof decides scope.** What the client can evidence is the ceiling on what any of these can
   claim.
