# What not to do — the content marketing kill list

Load this first in **factcheck** mode. Evidence in `research-synthesis.md`; `[sN]` resolves via
`_research/sources.json`.

**One distinction governs this whole file.** `copywriting-advisor` can say the eight-second
attention span *is fabricated* because it holds sources tracing the claim and failing to find
its origin. This corpus is usually in a weaker position: the folklore pass (Q17) returned mostly
statistics round-ups rather than debunkings, so for most claims below the honest verdict is
**"no traceable primary source in this corpus"**, not "proven false". Say it that way. Claiming
more certainty than the evidence supports is the same error as the folklore itself.

---

## Part 1 — Numbers with no traceable source

### "Buyers are 57% of the way through the purchase journey before contacting sales"

**No traceable primary source.** No disclosed method, no independent replication.

**The tell is in the claim itself:** the figure circulates as 57%, 60%, 67% and 70% depending on
the retelling, and the different versions are attributed to the same original. A real finding
does not drift.

**Say:** "That figure traces to consultancy publications rather than a controlled study, and the
percentage shifts between retellings, which is usually a sign there was never a measurement.
What is defensible is that buyers self-educate before contacting vendors. How much is not known."

### "Content marketing costs 62% less and generates 3x the leads"

**No traceable primary source.** It appears only in round-ups. One round-up asserts the "3x more
leads" half while itself remaining a compiled list with no primary link.

**Say:** "There is no study behind that. What the evidence supports is a positive but modest
average effect of owned social media on sales `[C]` [s6][s18] - direction, not a multiple."

### "The 80/20 rule" and "the 4-1-1 rule" for educational versus promotional content

**Presented everywhere as rules of thumb, nowhere as an empirical claim.** No source in this
corpus tests any ratio against any other.

**Say:** "Those are heuristics, not findings, and nobody has tested them against alternatives.
Set the mix from your funnel and your audience's awareness level, and we will say in the
document that is what it was based on."

### "Video generates 1,200% more shares than text and images combined"

**No traceable primary source, no methodological detail anywhere.**

**What is true:** emotion and arousal predict sharing; **information-focused content
significantly *reduced* sharing** in two independent field studies of video ads, except in risky
contexts `[C]`. That is a more useful finding than the fake one.

### "85% of video is watched without sound"

**Not traceable**, and separately, the short-form pass **failed to substantiate audio-off
viewing rates at all** `[C]`.

**Say:** "There is no source for that number. Caption anyway - captions have measured effects on
comprehension and retention `[C]` [s41], and accessibility requirements are normative `[C]`
[s62]. The reason to caption is not the statistic."

### "A buyer needs seven touches"

**Framed historically as a rule of thumb.** Meanwhile effective frequency *has* been
meta-analysed `[C]` [s99] and carryover effects likewise `[C]` [s108] - and neither produces a
universal number.

### "One video repurposes into 30 pieces"

**Workflow framing, never an empirical statistic.**

**The substantive objection is better than the sourcing one:** derivative quality is bounded by
the source asset. Thirty derivatives of a thin recording is thirty thin pieces.

### "Content half-life is X hours"

**A vendor coinage.** The real construct is attention decay, which is measured: attention fades
as novelty decays `[C]` [s20][s21][s26][s54], collective memory and attention decay follows a
universal form `[C]` [s126], and decay *profiles differ by mechanism* - exogenous bursts decay
faster than endogenous spread `[C]`. A single half-life number flattens all of that.

## Part 2 — Claims that are real but constantly misused

### The 95-5 rule

**Do not put this in the fabricated pile.** It is genuine work from the LinkedIn B2B Institute
and Ehrenberg-Bass. This corpus simply **does not contain it** - Q17 looked and found nothing
either way. Cite it from its actual source, not from here, and never cite Q17 against it.

### Pew, Reuters Institute and Ofcom figures

**Confirmed-tier, and constantly misapplied.** They measure **what a population consumes**.

- Supported: "X% of adults regularly get news on TikTok."
- **Not supported:** "video posts outperform text posts", "your audience is on TikTok",
  "we should move budget to TikTok".

Consumption data tells you where people are, never what performs for a specific business. This
is the most likely way this corpus gets misused.

### "Keep videos under six minutes"

**Real, and from an unexpected place.** The finding is from **6.9 million MOOC viewing
sessions** `[C]` - an education dataset, not marketing. It is strong evidence about instructional
video and weaker evidence about a brand film or a sales explainer.

**Say:** "That comes from educational video research at very large scale. It transfers well to
teaching content and less well to other jobs a video might do."

### Completion rate and short-form engagement

**Duration-biased by construction.** Shorter items achieve higher completion mechanically while
longer ones accumulate more watch time, and looping distorts both `[C]` [s31]. Feeds also carry
strong position bias `[C]` [s31].

**Never compare completion rates between videos of different lengths**, or treat a high
completion rate on a 7-second clip as a content quality signal.

### Email open rates

**Broken since 2021.** Apple Mail Privacy Protection preloads images through a proxy, registering
opens the recipient never performed `[P]`.

**Pre-2021 and post-2021 open rates are not comparable, and two lists with different Apple Mail
shares are not comparable to each other.** No peer-reviewed quantification of the inflation
exists here; the widely repeated "15-20%" is a practitioner opinion post.

### Attribution reports

**Attribution is not incrementality.** Observational models cannot reliably estimate incremental
impact `[C]` [s28], and across nearly **2,000 representative Meta campaigns** sophisticated
observational models produced **large errors against experimental ground truth** `[C]` [s28].

Never present an attribution report as a causal measurement of what content produced.

## Part 3 — Comparisons that are not interpretable

### Cross-platform view counts

**Refuse the comparison.** Per-platform view thresholds are absent from the research literature
`[C]` [s1] and exist only in platform documentation `[P*]` [s426][s427]. The counting rules
differ, so "40,000 views on Reels versus 12,000 on YouTube" is not a ratio, it is two different
measurements.

### Engagement rate between two accounts, or two vendor benchmark tables

**There is no single engagement-rate formula** `[C]` [s1]. Denominators vary - followers, reach,
impressions, views - and different denominators give different answers. "Engagements" has no
canonical component list either.

This is why vendor benchmark tables disagree with each other, and why quoting one at a client is
a liability.

### "Our competitor's content is more viral"

**Ask what the cascade sizes were first.** Apparent structural differences in diffusion between
content types or platforms **often reflect differences in cascade size**, and after matching on
size those differences diminish or reverse `[C]` [s14][s16].

## Part 4 — Craft-tier folklore

The craft passes generated their own unsourced numbers immediately, exactly as
`copywriting-advisor`'s did. **Anything numeric that appears only in the craft layer is
convention at best.** In particular, treat as unsourced unless a `[C]` or `[P*]` source is
produced:

- optimal posting times and days
- hashtag counts
- whether links in the post body suppress reach
- "ideal" character counts and word counts presented as performance thresholds
- follower-count thresholds for anything

**Do not treat a convention as a finding**, and do not let a client treat one as a finding
because it appeared in a spec file. The `[K]` tag is there so the distinction survives contact
with a deck.

## Part 5 — Things not to promise

- **A percentage lift.** See Q15. Nobody can forecast this, and the measurement stack most
  clients own could not detect it if it happened.
- **Virality.** Cascade size is famously hard to predict; structural virality and size are
  distinct dimensions `[C]` [s13].
- **That AI content is undetectable**, or that detection is reliable. Both are false: detection
  works but degrades under editing, domain shift and model updates, and humans are not reliably
  better than machines at the task `[C]` [s25][s30][s158][s162].
- **That refreshing a page restores its traffic.** The novelty model implies it should and **no
  study in this corpus tests the tactic.**
