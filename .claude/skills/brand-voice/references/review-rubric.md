# Review Rubric — auditing an existing brand voice guide

Audit mode. The client has a voice guide, a tone-of-voice page, a messaging doc, or a style
guide. The job is to say whether a writer could actually use it, then fix what is worth fixing.

**This rubric is ours.** No validated voice-guide audit instrument exists in the corpus. Present
it as a structured judgment, not a published scoring model.

---

## The lens

One question runs through every row: **could a competent freelance writer who has never met
this client produce on-brand copy from this document?**

Most voice guides fail that test while looking complete. They describe a voice accurately
enough that the client recognises themselves, and still give a writer nothing to act on. The
gap between "recognises itself" and "can be executed" is what this audit measures.

The one evidenced position to hold: **a casual register is not automatically better.** Informal
brand communication can backfire `[C]` [s79]. A guide that mandates conversational warmth
without arguing it from audience and context is asserting, not deciding.

---

## The scorecard

Six rows. Each is **Strong / Workable / Weak / Missing**. Lead the deliverable with the table,
then justify every row.

| # | Dimension | Strong means |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Dimensions** | Scales with marked positions, and exclusions named at both ends. Not adjectives |
| 2 | **Tone shifts** | Named contexts the client actually writes in, each with what moves and what stays fixed |
| 3 | **Messaging** | Core message, pillars, and proof points that are facts rather than claims |
| 4 | **Vocabulary** | A never-use list containing their real filler, not just profanity and competitor names |
| 5 | **Mechanics** | Sentence and paragraph rules, person, tense, structural habits and prohibitions |
| 6 | **Proof** | Before-and-after rewrites of the client's own copy, each traceable to a rule |

**Scoring discipline.** Weak means present but unusable. Missing means absent. Do not soften.

**Calibration.** Rows 1 and 6 fail most often. The canonical failures are "friendly but
professional" as a dimension set, and a guide with no rewrites at all, which means nobody has
ever tested whether it can be applied.

---

## Reading order

1. **Read the dimensions and ask what each one excludes.** If a dimension rules nothing out on
   either end, it is a midpoint, which is not a choice. "Friendly but professional" excludes
   nothing and fits most B2B brands.
2. **Try to apply it.** Take a paragraph of the client's copy and rewrite it using only the
   document. Where you have to guess, the guide has a hole. This is the fastest way to find
   rows 1 and 5 failures and it takes five minutes.
3. **Check the proof points are facts.** "Industry-leading expertise" is a claim. A named
   outcome, a number, a credential, or a guarantee is a proof point.
4. **Check the never-use list for their actual filler.** Solutions, leverage, seamless, robust,
   cutting-edge, passionate, journey. If none of their real habits appear, nobody read their
   copy before writing the guide.
5. **Check the rewrites exist and are theirs.** Invented examples prove nothing. If every
   rewrite is obviously better in ways no rule explains, the guide is incomplete and the
   examples were done on instinct.
6. **Compare the guide against live copy.** A guide nobody follows is a different failure from
   a guide that cannot be followed, and the fixes differ. Adoption is an organizational problem
   `[C]` [s45] [s50], not a documentation one.

---

## Then fix what is worth fixing

Rank by leverage. Rows 1 and 6 are almost always highest, because everything else inherits from
the dimensions and nothing is verifiable without the rewrites.

Rewrite the two or three highest-leverage pieces **in full** so the client sees the standard,
rather than describing what a better version would contain. For a voice document specifically,
showing beats telling: a rewritten dimension with its exclusions is worth more than a paragraph
explaining why the current one is weak.

**If the guide is really a style guide**: oxford commas, capitalisation, date formats, with no
voice in it at all, say so. That is a useful document and a different one. Offer to build the
voice layer.

**If the problem is that nobody follows it**, the fix is not a better document. Route the
conversation to adoption: who writes, what they reach for, and why the guide does not answer
the question they actually have `[P]` [s181] [s190].
