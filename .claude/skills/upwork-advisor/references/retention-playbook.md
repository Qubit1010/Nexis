## Why retainers win, on pure math

Upwork's fee is tiered per client. It **drops to 5% after $10,000 billed with that one client**
[Q8]. So the same revenue earns materially more through one deep relationship than through churn,
before counting the stability or the zero acquisition cost.

Stack the effects:
- No connects spent [Q4]
- No proposal time
- Fee falls to 5% past the $10K mark [Q8]
- Long relationships feed JSS positively, which feeds search ranking [Q2][Q7]

A retained client is worth multiples of a new one at the same contract value. This is the single
strongest argument in the corpus for changing where effort goes.

## The target portfolio shape

**2 long-term clients + 3-5 rotating project clients** [Q8].

The reasoning: two anchors stabilize income, the rotating set prevents over-reliance on any single
source. One anchor is fragile; four anchors usually means underpricing.

Diagnose Aleem's actual mix against this. Too few anchors is a stability problem. All anchors and no
rotation is a growth and pricing problem.

## Who to keep, who to fire

**Protect JSS by being selective and firing bad-fit clients early** [Q8]. That's stated as strategy,
not just comfort: JSS is the top-weighted ranking signal [Q2] and a sustained 90% over 13 of 16
weeks gates Top Rated [Q7]. One bad client can cost weeks of badge eligibility.

Keep: clients whose work compounds (repeat scope, referrals, case-study value), pay promptly, and
give clean private feedback.

Fire early: scope-creepers, anxiety-signal clients from the job post stage (`job-search-playbook.md`),
and anyone whose contract is trending toward a dispute. Early and clean beats late and formal.

**First five contracts matter disproportionately** — focus hard on delivery quality there to unlock
Top Rated [Q8].

## Raising rates on existing clients

- **30 days' notice**, framed factually as a market adjustment, no apology [Q6]
- New rates apply to **all new proposals immediately** [Q6]
- On pushback: **cut scope, never the rate** [Q6]
