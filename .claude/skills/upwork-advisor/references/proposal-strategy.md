## Diagnose before prescribing

Get the numbers first. The fix is completely different depending on where the funnel breaks:

| Symptom | Benchmark | Diagnosis | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reply rate **under ~15%** | Platform avg ~15%, top quartile 22-30% [Q5] | Targeting or first-two-lines | Tighten job selection (`job-search-playbook.md`), rewrite the opener |
| Reply rate fine, **win rate low** | Top tier 30-50% [Q5] | Fit or pricing downstream | Wrong jobs, or rate/scope mismatch (`strategy-2026.md` Q6 material) |
| Both fine, **too few contracts** | 10-30 proposals to first contract when new [Q5] | Volume against a working system | Now volume helps. Only now |
| Sending lots, winning little | — | **Volume is the problem, not the answer** [Q4] | Cut proposal count, raise the fit threshold to 7.5/10 |

If Aleem doesn't know his reply rate, that's the first thing to fix — you can't tune a system
nobody's measuring.

## The four levers, in order of impact

### 1. Speed (dominant)
The first **5 minutes** matter most. Miss it and target the **12-15 minute** rebound; avoid the
**5-10 minute valley** [Q5]. Never bid on posts 24-48h+ old or with 20+ proposals [Q5][Q3].

Strategic implication: a narrow saved search that fires early beats a great proposal sent late.

### 2. The first two lines
This is the preview text the client sees before opening [Q5]. It must:
- **Diagnose a specific problem from the brief** [Q5]
- Avoid self-centered "I" statements and generic greetings [Q5]
- Consider an opening **question** — worth up to **+479% reply rate** vs a statement [Q5]

### 3. Structure — the four-part frame
hook proving you read the brief → brief proof of similar work **with numbers** → clear approach →
**open-ended question as the CTA** [Q5].

**Avoid bulleted process lists** — they signal the client could do this themselves [Q5].
Length: **150-300 words**; length is not the differentiator [Q5].

### 4. Targeting
Verified payment, hiring history, under 20 proposals [Q5][Q3]. See the triage rubric in
`job-search-playbook.md`.

## The AI-detection reality

AI-generated proposals **underperform and are actively filtered** by both the platform and clients
[Q5]. This is the evidence base behind `upwork-proposal-generator`'s "Sound Human, Not AI" section —
that section is not stylistic preference, it's a response to documented filtering.

Strategic consequence: proposal volume via automation is a losing play in 2026. Fewer, genuinely
hand-tuned proposals to better-fit jobs is the only structure the evidence supports.

## Connects as an acquisition budget

Reframe every proposal as a purchase [Q4]:

```
cost per contract = (connects per proposal x $0.15) / win rate
```

At 6-16 connects ($0.90-$2.40) and a 30% win rate, a contract costs roughly $3-8 in connects. That's
trivial against a $1,000 job, which is the actual argument for being *pickier*, not busier: the
constraint is your time and reply-rate quality, not connect cost.

**Boost only** at 8+ fit score, **$1,000+** job value, with directly relevant proof [Q4].
