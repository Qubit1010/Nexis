## By section

### Mission, vision, values
- Mission statement to financial performance: **effect still unclear after 20+ years of
  research** `[C]` [s58]. Effectiveness is subject to substantial skepticism `[C]` [s57].
- Vision to **employee** work performance: positive association, one hospital study `[C]` [s2].
- Mission statements in practice: subjective, vague, inward-looking, rarely give employees
  usable focus `[C]` [s59].
- **Deliverable implication:** justify this section on decision clarity and delegation, never
  on revenue. If a client wants ROI on a values exercise, tell them the evidence is not there.

### Market sizing
- Founder revenue forecasts overshoot by **22%** `[C]` [s64]. Founder survival expectations
  **barely distinguish survivors from exits** `[C]` [s67]. Investors cannot filter the bias
  out `[C]` [s62].
- Method: TAM/SAM/SOM nested, top-down narrows a published total, bottom-up multiplies
  accounts by revenue per customer, **triangulate both and validate with real buyers** `[P]`
  [s94] [s74] [s79].
- Investors judge clarity of customer and route to them, not the size of the number `[P]` [s74].
- **Deliverable implication:** build bottom-up, cross-check top-down, show every assumption,
  and discount client-supplied inputs explicitly.

### Target customer
- Demographic and geographic bases are criticized for **failing to predict behaviour** `[C]` [s10].
- **Segment stability must be evaluated, not assumed** `[C]` [s11].
- Segment-level profitability models are sufficient for resource allocation; you do not need
  individual-level LTV `[C]` [s12].
- **Deliverable implication:** segment on behaviour and value. A persona with demographics and
  a stock photo is the weakest deliverable in this category.

### Competitive position
- Variance in profitability: year **2%**, industry **19%**, corporate parent **4%**,
  business-specific **32%** `[C]` [s50]. Split varies by effect-size measure `[C]` [s51].
- Industry structure is **dynamic**, not a snapshot `[C]` [s1].
- **Deliverable implication:** read the industry to find the constraint, then spend the
  client's attention on the 32% they control.

### Positioning and UVP
- **Contested.** Meta-analysis supports differentiation to financial performance `[C]` [s15];
  differentiation is more sustainable than cost leadership `[C]` [s16].
- Against: Romaniuk, Sharp and Ehrenberg challenge the central importance of perceived brand
  differentiation `[C]` [s20]. Optimal-distinctiveness findings are **mixed** `[C]` [s19].
  Unique resources do not automatically convert to profit once rivals respond `[C]` [s21].
- **Deliverable implication:** differentiation in the offer and the economics has support.
  Differentiation that lives only in messaging is what the counter-evidence targets.

### Business model
- BMI to performance: **robust across 147 studies, 27 countries** `[C]` [s28].
- **Life-cycle dependent** — matters most early, less later `[C]` [s3].
- Breadth of reconfiguration has an **inverted U-shape** with performance `[C]` [s27].
- **Refining an existing model pays off**; a dynamic environment does not automatically
  demand more innovation `[C]` [s25]. Novelty is **not sufficient** for performance `[C]` [s31].
- **Deliverable implication:** most clients should sharpen the model they have. Recommend
  reinvention only when the diagnosis demands it.
- **No margin or revenue-model benchmarks exist in this corpus.** Do not invent them.

### Financial forecast
- **78-80%** one-year establishment survival `[C]` [s8], against the folklore "90% fail",
  which is practitioner-sourced `[P]` [s7] [s36] and measures something different.
- **Short-term revenue volatility strongly predicts exit**, 6,578 ventures over 10 years `[C]` [s32].
- Failure causes **differ by firm age** `[C]` [s35]. New venture sales approximate a random
  walk `[C]` [s37].
- **No CAC, LTV, LTV:CAC or gross-margin benchmarks in this corpus.** State the concept,
  refuse the number.

### Strategy diagnosis
- **Formal strategic planning improves financial performance in small firms**, by
  meta-analysis `[C]` [s45]. Supported more broadly `[C]` [s40] [s43], though earlier work
  found genuinely mixed results confounded by small samples `[C]` [s44].
- **Strategy alignment** is the bridge between formulation and implementation `[C]` [s47].
- The **"50-90% of strategies fail"** family of claims is not established `[C]` [s41] [s49].
- **Deliverable implication:** [s45] is the evidence that this deliverable is worth buying.
  Lead with it when a client asks whether strategy work pays.

### Audience persona (content and search)
- Personas afford **faster task completion** than an analytics system on identical data, in a
  **34-participant** controlled experiment `[C]` [s255]. That is the measured benefit:
  understanding, not prediction.
- The same paper notes a **lack of rigorous empirical research** evaluating personas against
  other methods `[C]` [s255]. Thin evidence base, honestly reported.
- The persona literature criticizes itself for **flat, assumption-based** profiles `[C]`
  [s257] [s256]. Data-driven construction is the corrective `[C]` [s252] [s254].
- **Transparency about how a persona was built measurably improves how it is received**
  `[C]` [s253]. This is why every line carries an evidence marker.
- Customers describe needs in **layman's terms**, not specification terms, and closing that
  gap needs deliberate method `[C]` [s260]. Mining user-generated content and reviews is the
  established way `[C]` [s260] [s251].
- Query intent is genuinely hard to infer, with a real share of queries landing in "unknown"
  intent even for major e-commerce engines `[C]` [s259] [s258]. Do not guess intent from a
  keyword string; get the phrasing from real sources.
- **Deliverable implication:** the language and question sections are the load-bearing parts.
  Demographics are scaffolding `[C]` [s10]. Never claim the persona lifts traffic or
  conversion, and never invent a quote.

## Numbers this corpus does NOT have

Refuse these rather than estimating. Run the live fallback if a client genuinely needs one.

- CAC, LTV, or a target LTV:CAC ratio
- Gross margin benchmarks by business model or industry
- Runway, burn rate, or how much to raise
- Sector-by-sector TAM figures or market growth rates
- Any conversion rate, close rate, or churn benchmark
- Pricing multiples or valuation benchmarks
