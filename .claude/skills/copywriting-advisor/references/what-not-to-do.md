# What not to do — the copywriting kill list

Load this in factcheck mode, and before delivering any copy.

Two halves: **claims that are not true**, and **claims that create legal exposure**. The second
half matters more, because being wrong about the fold costs a client nothing and an
unsubstantiated testimonial can cost them a lot.

Full evidence in `research-synthesis.md`. `[C]` confirmed, `[P]` practitioner.

---

## Part 1 — Statistics that are not real

### "You have 8 seconds before you lose them"

**Fabricated.** No primary study supports it `[P]` [s154]. The BBC traced the "12 seconds in
2000, 8 seconds now, less than a goldfish" formulation and found no source `[P]` [s159]. Half
the UK public believes it `[P]` [s152].

**What is true:** attention on screens has measurably shortened over two decades `[C]` [s3] -
but that measures how long someone stays on a screen before switching, which is a different
construct and is not eight seconds.

**Say:** "That number isn't real. The underlying concern is - people switch faster than they
used to - which is an argument for getting to the point, not for a countdown."

### "Nobody reads below the fold"

**False as stated.** NN/g eye-tracking: content above the fold receives **57% of viewing time**,
the second screenful about **17%**, the remaining **26%** spread in a long tail `[C]` [s259].
People scroll. Attention is front-loaded, which is a different claim with different
implications.

**Say:** "They scroll, but the top of the page is worth roughly three times the second
screenful. Put the load-bearing message high, don't refuse to write below it."

### "The $300 million button proves you should remove registration"

**A single uncontrolled case, self-reported by the consultant** `[P]` [s208], with the 45% and
$300M figures appearing in retellings `[P]` [s198][s199]. No published methodology, no
baseline, no replication, and the dollar figure is annualised estimate. **No confirmed source
exists for it.**

**Say:** "It's a real engagement, not a myth, but it's an illustration of checkout friction -
never an effect size to plan against."

### "Personalised subject lines lift open rates by 26%"

**No confirmed source.** Vendor-published `[P]` [s142][s144][s148]. Real academic work on
subject-line personalisation exists `[P]` [s145][s147] but its published versions are not in
this corpus, so **no personalisation lift figure here is confirmed-tier.**

Compounding it: **open rate has meant something different since 2021.** Apple Mail Privacy
Protection pre-fetches images for protected users, registering opens regardless of reading.
Any comparison spanning 2021 compares two different measurements.

**Say:** "That's a vendor number from a company selling email software, and open rate stopped
being a clean metric in 2021. Measure your own list and use clicks."

### "Long copy outsells short copy"

**No confirmed support.** A well-controlled direct-mail experiment found **no significant
difference in response rates by readability level** `[C]` [s24]. Landing page content volume
correlates **negatively** with conversion `[C]` [s1]. A paid-search field experiment across
280,877 observations is titled "achieving more by saying less" `[C]` [s28].

The honest version is an inverted U `[C]` [s88]: too little information fails too.

**Say:** "Length follows what the reader needs to decide. Whoever wants it longer carries the
burden of proof."

### "Red buttons convert better" / button colour rules

**Not supported.** A charity donation-button design experiment found **no significant effect**
on transactional trust `[C]` [s81]. The corpus contains no confirmed source establishing a
colour effect on conversion.

**What does have support:** repeating the headline message verbatim on the button raised
clickthrough in a field study of 956 visitors `[C]` [s2].

### "People only read 20% of a page"

**Not in sources.** Do not repeat it, and do not repeat the Nielsen-attributed version from
memory. Use the NN/g viewing-time distribution `[C]` [s259] instead, which is real and
measured.

### "Switch to loss framing for a big lift"

**Oversold.** Goal and message framing show a **lack of consistent evidence** despite
considerable study `[C]` [s11]. Gain-framed sun-protection messages: k = 33, N = 4,168, **no
significant difference** `[C]` [s111]. Across **1,149 studies of 30 message variations**,
differences between message forms **don't make much practical difference** to persuasiveness
`[C]` [s107].

**Say:** "Framing is a smaller lever than it's sold as. Fix the offer, the proof and the
clarity first."

### "Always benefits, never features"

**Moderated, not universal.** Whether to lead with attributes or benefits depends on construal
level and how near and concrete the decision is `[C]` [s41][s45][s10]. Feature and benefit
sentences affect memory differently `[C]` [s46].

**Say:** "For a near-term, concrete purchase, stripping the specifications out can cost you
the sale."

### "Mirror your customer's language"

**Supported, with a real backfire condition.** Language style matching improves perceived
review quality and helpfulness `[C]` [s35][s37]. But the **Language Backfire Effect**: switching
language to serve customers *decreased* satisfaction **even when switching into the customer's
own language**, via perceived identity threat, also reducing word-of-mouth and repurchase
`[C]` [s36].

**Say:** "Mine their vocabulary. Don't perform it."

### "This change will lift conversion by X%"

**Unknowable in advance.** Roughly **30% of tested ideas improve metrics at all** in mature
programs `[P]` [s307]. Tests need users **in the thousands** minimum `[P]` [s302]. Stopping a
test when it looks good is optional stopping, a known invalidation `[C]` [s271], and is the
mechanism behind wins that do not persist.

**Never promise a percentage.** Argue the change on its merits.

### "You have about 2 seconds before someone scrolls past"

**Unsourced `[K]`.** Surfaced by the craft passes with no primary citation. Structurally
identical to the 8-second myth: a plausible-sounding interval, endlessly repeated, traceable
to nobody.

**Say:** same answer as the 8-second claim. Attention is front-loaded; the interval is made up.

### "78% of marketing agencies now use generative AI"

**Unverified `[K]`.** Repeated across multiple 2026 craft sources and attributed to Statista,
but the primary table is never linked in any of them. A number that only ever appears as a
citation of a citation.

**Say:** it may well be roughly true, but nobody in these sources has seen the underlying
data. Do not put it in a client deck as fact.

### "Microcopy is the 3-5 most-read words on your interface"

**Vendor assertion `[K]`.** No measurement behind it.

### Any specific platform character limit

**Zero confirmed sources** `[P]` [s153][s155][s157][s160][s161]. Verify against the platform's
own documentation and record the date. Truncation point matters more than field limit.

## Part 2 — Legal exposure, not style

Primary sources, US. **Not legal advice** - flag to the client and their counsel.

| Do not | Why |
|---|---|
| Write a testimonial the customer did not say | 16 CFR Part 255 governs endorsements as interpretations of Section 5 of the FTC Act `[C]` [s263][s265] |
| Publish claims made through endorsements without substantiation | The advertiser must possess and rely on adequate substantiation for endorsement claims to the same standard as direct claims `[P]` [s301], per the FTC substantiation policy `[C]` [s264] |
| Hide a material connection between advertiser and endorser | Material connections must be fully disclosed `[C]` [s283]; disclosure measurably changes consumer response `[C]` [s284][s289] |
| Commission, incentivise or suppress reviews to fake a rating | The Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials prohibits identified practices and is actively enforced `[P]` [s311][s314]; fake reviews are regulated in the US, UK and EU `[C]` [s286] |
| Assume only literal words count | The FTC assesses the advertisement **as a whole**, including implied claims `[C]` [s288][s285] |
| Run a countdown that resets, or "only 3 left" that is untrue | No confirmed source on timer-specific rules retrieved, so **do not cite a specific rule**. The general deception principle applies `[C]` [s288]: it is a representation that cannot be substantiated |

Beyond the law, manufactured urgency has a measured commercial cost: supply-driven scarcity
can reduce perceived retailer sincerity `[C]` [s121], limited-time offers reverse into negative
effects under reactance `[C]` [s125], and pressure nudges affect returns as well as purchases
`[C]` [s127].

## Part 3 — Craft failures

- **Adjectives standing in for proof.** "World-class", "innovative", "seamless". If the
  opposite is absurd, the word carries no information.
- **Needless complexity.** Complex vocabulary lowers judged intelligence `[C]` [s136]; plain
  redrafting measurably improves comprehension `[C]` [s140].
- **Competing CTAs of equal weight.** One page, one action.
- **Proof hosted only where the seller controls it.** Review effects on sales are significantly
  larger on third-party sites `[C]` [s114].
- **Answering objections only in a bottom-of-page FAQ**, where the hesitant reader never
  reaches.
- **AI tells.** Run `blog-writer/references/human-tone-rules.md` rather than restating it here.
