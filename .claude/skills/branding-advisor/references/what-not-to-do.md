# What Not To Do — the kill list

Claims and habits this skill family refuses. Run every deliverable through this before sending.

Most entries exist because the branding default is confidently wrong, not merely unhelpful.
Branding carries more unsourced folklore than any other subject in this repo: the numbers that
circulate in agency decks are largely vendor surveys with the attribution stripped, or figures
with no origin at all. Repeating them is the fastest way to lose a client who checks.

---

## Tier 1 — Numbers we refuse to repeat

| Claim | Why it dies | Say instead |
|---|---|---|
| "Colour increases brand recognition by 80%" | Two independent provenance traces conclude it is untraceable to any credible primary source and should be treated as a myth `[P]` [s144] [s147] | "That figure has no primary source. What is established is that hue maps to brand personality, red to excitement, blue to competence `[C]` [s107], and that colour *fit* to the brand matters more than hue alone `[C]` [s104]." |
| "Consistent branding increases revenue by 23%" | A vendor survey, not peer-reviewed. Not present at confirmed tier | "Consistency is standard practice, but whether guidelines measurably improve it is only anecdotally evidenced `[P]` [s178]. One paper explicitly questions the consistency dogma `[C]` [s46]." |
| "X% of rebrands fail" | No primary data in the rebranding literature here | "There is no credible failure rate. What is evidenced is the mechanism: high-identifying customers react most negatively to identity change `[C]` [s29]." |
| "Stories are 22x more memorable than facts" | Not traceable in this corpus | "Narrative persuasion has meta-analytic support `[C]` [s87] [s88]. The multiplier does not." |
| "Typeface choice lifts positive response by 13%" | Secondhand vendor claim `[P]` [s160] | "Typefaces carry semantic associations that transfer to the message `[C]` [s114]. The 13% is a vendor figure." |
| "Baskerville is more believable than Comic Sans" | Reported secondhand `[P]` [s189], widely repeated, unverified here | Cite the typeface-semantics literature instead `[C]` [s114] [s118]. |
| "85% of B2B buyers shortlist brands they recognize" | Vendor statistic `[P]` [s151] | "Brand equity does link to acquisition, retention and margin `[C]` [s55]. That specific number is a vendor's." |
| Any ROI figure for a logo change or rebrand | No "X% improvement" statistic about logo effects is traceable to a primary study | "Direction is evidenced, magnitude is not. Nobody can honestly tell you what a new logo is worth in revenue." |
| A brand awareness benchmark for their category | Not in this corpus | "I do not have a sourced benchmark. We can measure your baseline and track movement instead." |
| A font licensing cost | Not in this corpus, and licences vary per foundry and per use | "Web, desktop and app use are licensed separately. I will check this typeface specifically rather than quote a figure." |

## Tier 2 — Method failures

- **Never present Jungian archetypes as evidence-based.** Agency sources claim the 12 archetypes
  transcend cultures with no empirical support `[P]` [s169]. They are a creative alignment
  device. The peer-reviewed literature that touches them *interrogates* them `[C]` [s77] rather
  than validating them. Aaker's personality scale `[C]` [s69] is a different kind of object and
  the two must not be blurred together to borrow credibility.
- **Never assert that a clearly positioned brand outperforms an ambiguous one.** Practitioners
  claim it universally; **no causal evidence exists in this corpus**. Positioning effectiveness
  is measurable as a construct `[C]` [s62], which is not the same claim.
- **Never resolve distinctiveness versus differentiation.** Both have evidence `[C]` [s1] [s2]
  [s24] versus `[C]` [s61] [s63] [s64]. Flattening a live argument to make a tidier deliverable
  is a false certainty. Explain that they answer different questions.
- **Never recommend a rebrand by reflex.** Identity change carries measured recognition cost
  `[C]` [s32] and the most loyal customers object most `[C]` [s29]. Run the triage in
  `diagnosis-playbooks.md` and distinguish refresh from rebrand before quoting.
- **Never default to "make it more conversational".** Informal brand communication can backfire
  `[C]` [s79]. Voice must be argued as a fit to audience and context, not asserted as best
  practice.
- **Never claim a serif-versus-sans legibility rule.** Genuinely contested, and practitioner
  reviews conclude no universal rule holds `[P]` [s199] [s207].
- **Never assert contrast passes.** Compute it. A ratio is a measurement, not a judgment, and
  brand colours fail it more often than clients expect.
- **Never treat a guidelines document as the solution to inconsistency.** Adoption is an
  organizational problem: employee response is the hinge `[C]` [s45] [s50], and print-first
  documents that ignore digital are a documented cause of non-adoption `[P]` [s181] [s190]. A
  more beautiful PDF does not fix it.
- **Never invent a customer quote, a brand perception, or a competitor's brand-recall figure.**
  If it was not measured or said, it does not go in the document.
- **Never present founder-brand work as evidence-backed.** One confirmed source `[C]` [s5]. Use
  the founder-story authenticity finding `[C]` [s91], which is real, and label the rest as
  judgment.
- **Never state key-person concentration risk as a research finding.** It is a reasoned
  argument. Make it, and label it.
- **Never report a small-sample survey as a percentage.** A 20-response brand survey is a
  directional signal. Sample-size requirements are not in this corpus.

## Tier 3 — Framework honesty

These are useful and we use them. They are **not** evidence and must never be cited as if they
were.

| Framework | Status here |
|---|---|
| Aaker brand identity planning model | Practitioner `[P]` [s140]. A structuring device. No empirical test in this corpus |
| Kapferer brand identity prism | Practitioner `[P]` [s143]. Same |
| Keller CBBE pyramid | Practitioner `[P]` [s154]. The *equity* construct beneath it is well researched `[C]` [s36] [s39]; the pyramid layout is not the research |
| Jungian 12 archetypes | **No empirical basis** `[P]` [s169]. Creative device only |
| Aaker brand personality scale | **Genuinely peer-reviewed** `[C]` [s69]. Cross-cultural structure varies `[C]` [s73] |
| StoryBrand and similar narrative frameworks | Practitioner. The underlying narrative transportation research is real `[C]` [s92]; the commercial framework is not tested |
| Perceptual maps | Practitioner `[P]` [s197]. A conversation structure, not a measurement |
| Distinctive Brand Assets framework | Sits on real empirical generalizations `[C]` [s7] [s24]. The packaged framework is practitioner |
| Voice dimension sliders | Practitioner convention. No corpus evidence. Still the best available format because it forces exclusions |
| Brand guidelines as a document type | Practitioner. Contents are conventional, effectiveness only anecdotally evidenced `[P]` [s178] |

## Tier 4 — Deliverable smells

Signals the document has drifted into branding theatre.

- A brand strategy where swapping the client's name for a competitor's would change nothing.
- Personality traits that are three positive adjectives no company would reject. If the
  opposite is obviously undesirable, the trait carries no information.
- A voice guide made of adjectives instead of scales. "Friendly but professional" excludes
  nothing.
- A voice guide with no before-and-after rewrites, so nobody can tell whether it is applicable.
- Values with no trade-off attached. A value that costs nothing is not a value.
- A colour palette with five colours of equal weight and no usage rules.
- A palette presented without a single computed contrast ratio.
- A typography section that names two fonts and stops, with no scale, weights, fallbacks or
  licence check.
- A logo recommendation so vague that three designers would produce three unrelated marks.
- A "what this rules out" section that is empty, hedged, or lists only things nobody wanted.
- A founder brand document that is really a posting schedule.
- Any section that would read identically if the client's business were different.

## Tier 5 — Tone

- No em dashes in body text. No emojis.
- Never mention NexusPoint or Aleem inside a client document.
- Do not launder a vendor's marketing number into a fact by dropping the attribution. If the
  attribution makes it look weak, that is information, not a formatting problem.
- Do not use a peer-reviewed citation to support a claim it does not make. Several findings
  here establish that a relationship was *studied*, not how large it is.
- Do not fill an evidence gap with a plausible number. On this subject, a plausible number is
  usually how the folklore started.
- "I do not have a sourced figure for that" is a better deliverable than a confident
  invention, and on branding it is the whole reason this skill is worth more than a generic
  answer.
