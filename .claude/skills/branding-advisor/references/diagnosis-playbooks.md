# Diagnosis Playbooks

Clients almost never arrive with a correctly named brand problem. They arrive with a symptom
and a proposed solution, and the proposed solution is usually a logo.

This file turns the symptom into a diagnosis and routes it. **Diagnose before quoting.** The
most expensive mistake in brand work is executing the engagement the client asked for when a
cheaper or different one was the real answer.

---

## The five root causes

Nearly every brand complaint reduces to one of these. They have different fixes and different
prices, and they are frequently confused for one another.

| # | Root cause | What it actually is |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **No position** | The business has not decided who it is for or what it is against. Nothing downstream can be consistent because there is nothing to be consistent with. |
| 2 | **Position exists, never articulated** | The founder knows it. It is in their head, not in any document, so nobody else can execute it. |
| 3 | **Articulated, never systematised** | There is a strategy doc. There is no voice spec or visual system, so every execution re-invents. |
| 4 | **Systematised, not followed** | Guidelines exist. Nobody uses them, or they are unusable. This is a governance problem wearing a design problem's clothes. |
| 5 | **Followed, but wrong** | The system is coherent and consistently applied, and it is aimed at the wrong audience or says the wrong thing. The rarest, and the only one where a rebrand is the honest answer. |

**The diagnostic question that separates 4 from 3:** ask to see the guidelines. If they exist
and are being ignored, ask one person who ignores them why. The answer is almost always that
the guidelines do not answer the question they actually have.

## Symptom table

| What the client says | Likely cause | Check this first | Route to |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Our brand feels inconsistent" | 3 or 4 | Do written voice and visual specs exist? Are they usable? | `brand-visual` + `brand-voice` |
| "We need a new logo" | 1, 2 or 5 | Ask what problem the logo is meant to solve. Usually reveals a position problem. | `brand-strategy` first |
| "We look dated" | 3 or 5 | Compare against category norms, not against fashion | `brand-visual` |
| "We blend in with competitors" | 1 or 5 | Put five competitor sites side by side with theirs, covering the logos | `brand-strategy` |
| "Our messaging is all over the place" | 2 or 3 | Read their site, deck and social in one sitting | `brand-voice` |
| "Different writers sound different" | 3 | Is there a voice spec, and does it contain scales or adjectives? | `brand-voice` |
| "People don't understand what we do" | 1 or 2 | Read their homepage headline aloud to someone outside the industry | `brand-strategy` then `brand-voice` |
| "We're not premium enough" | 1 or 5 | Check whether pricing, delivery and proof actually support premium | `brand-strategy` |
| "We need brand guidelines" | 3 | Ask who will use them and for what. Scope follows the answer. | `brand-visual` (assembles) |
| "Our founder wants to be more visible" | separate track | Is the personal brand meant to feed or replace the company brand? | `brand-strategy` (founder subject) |
| "We're rebranding" | any | Which of the five is it? A rebrand only fixes 5. | diagnose before agreeing |

## Rebrand triage

Asked most often, and the one where a wrong yes costs the client the most. Rebranding
discards accumulated recognition, and the identity a business has now is an asset even when it
is ugly.

Work down this list. Stop at the first that applies.

1. **Legal or structural forcing function**: trademark conflict, merger, name that cannot be
   registered or domained, name that is offensive or unusable in a new market. Rebrand is
   forced. Proceed.
2. **The name actively misdescribes the business**: they sold the product the name refers to,
   or the name locks them into a geography or category they have left. Strong case.
3. **Genuine cause 5**: the identity is coherent and consistently applied and is aimed at the
   wrong audience. Real case, but verify it is not cause 1 in disguise. Cause 1 businesses
   often *believe* they are cause 5.
4. **Reputational contamination**: the existing brand carries damage that outlives any fix.
   Rebrand is a containment decision, not a design one.
5. **Everything else**: new marketing director, board fatigue, "it feels tired", a competitor
   did one. **Not a rebrand case.** These are refresh, systematise, or do-nothing cases.

**Refresh versus rebrand.** These are different products at different prices and the
distinction is worth holding firm on:

- **Refresh** keeps the name and the core mark's recognisability. Type, palette, layout,
  photography and voice can all change. Recognition survives.
- **Rebrand** replaces the name or the mark. Recognition resets to zero and has to be re-bought.

If the diagnosis is 3 or 4, the client wanted a rebrand and needs a system. Say so directly,
including that it costs less. That conversation earns more trust than agreeing does.

## Founder brand triage

The 2026 pattern is founder brand and company brand converging. Before starting, settle three
questions, because they change the deliverable:

1. **Feed or replace?** Is the founder's visibility a channel into the company brand, or is the
   company becoming an extension of the person? Agency and consultancy businesses often want
   the second and have not said so.
2. **What happens if they stop?** If the founder going quiet would materially damage the
   business, that is a concentration risk. Name it in the deliverable. Do not treat visibility
   as costless.
3. **Where do they diverge?** The founder can hold positions the company should not, and the
   company needs claims the founder cannot personally make. Define the separation before
   writing either voice, or the two documents will contradict each other.

Route the identity work to `brand-strategy` and `brand-voice` with subject set to founder.
Route content pillars, cadence, and platform choice to `marketing-advisor` and
`content-engine`, those are distribution decisions, not identity ones.

## What to do when the diagnosis is "nothing is wrong"

Sometimes the brand is fine and the business problem is elsewhere: the offer, the pricing, the
sales process, the traffic. Brand work will not fix any of those and will absorb budget that
should go to them.

Say so. Route to `strategic-foundation` for offer and positioning economics,
`marketing-advisor` for channel and demand, `sales-playbook` for conversion.

Declining an engagement that would not have worked is the highest-value thing this skill does,
and it is the reason the diagnosis step exists at all.
