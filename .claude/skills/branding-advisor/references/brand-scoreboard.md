# Brand Scoreboard

**Default load.** The evidence, compressed. Read this before answering anything; go to
`research-synthesis.md` only when you need the surrounding argument.

**Read the second half first if you are about to quote a number.** This corpus is unusual: its
most valuable content is the list of things it refuses to assert. Branding carries more
unsourced folklore than any other subject in this repo, and the fastest way to lose a
technically literate client is to open with a statistic they can trace to nothing.

## Numbers this corpus does NOT have

**Never supply these from memory.** Each one is either folklore, a vendor survey, or a
genuinely open question. Saying so is the correct answer.

| Asked for | Reality |
|---|---|
| "Colour increases brand recognition by 80%" | **No traceable primary source.** Two independent traces call it a myth `[P]` [s144] [s147] |
| "Consistent branding increases revenue by 23%" | Vendor survey. Not present at confirmed tier in this corpus |
| "X% of rebrands fail" | No primary data anywhere in Q12 |
| "Stories are 22x more memorable than facts" | Not traceable |
| "Typeface choice lifts response by 13%" | Secondhand vendor claim `[P]` [s160] |
| "Baskerville is more believable than Comic Sans" | Secondhand `[P]` [s189]; widely repeated, unverified here |
| "85% of B2B buyers shortlist brands they recognize" | Vendor statistic `[P]` [s151] |
| ROI of a logo change / rebrand | **No traceable primary study.** Direction yes, magnitude no |
| Effect size for almost any finding above | Mostly not extractable. State direction only |
| Do clearly positioned brands outperform? | **No causal evidence** in this corpus. Practitioners assert it; none show data |
| Do brand guidelines improve consistency? | Only anecdotal evidence `[P]` [s178] [s248] |
| Brand tracking sample-size requirements | Not in sources. A 20-response survey is directional, not a percentage |
| Font licensing costs | Not in sources. Check per typeface; never quote from memory |
| Category benchmark for brand awareness % | Not in sources |
| Key-person concentration risk for founder brands | A reasoned argument, **not** an evidenced finding |

## Evidence strength by subject

Where to be confident and where to hedge. Ordered strongest first.

| Subject | Strength | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Narrative and brand story | **Strong** | Meta-analytic support, clear moderators |
| Logo design | **Strong** | Deep literature, no magnitudes |
| Colour | **Strong on direction** | Well-studied, no effect sizes, myth-heavy surface |
| Naming | **Strong** | Sound symbolism and fluency are solid |
| Brand measurement | **Strong** | Mature; the gap is what SMBs can afford |
| Personality (the scale) | **Strong** | Real instrument, cross-cultural caveats |
| Rebranding risk | **Good** | Mechanism (identification) well evidenced |
| Distinctive assets | **Good, contested** | Preserve the argument, do not resolve it |
| Typography | **Mixed** | Semantics solid, legibility genuinely contested |
| Corporate visual identity | **Good** | Consistency dogma questioned; employees are the hinge |
| Voice and tone | **Weak on practice** | Frameworks unevidenced; the backfire finding is the real one |
| Positioning | **Weak on outcomes** | Machinery measurable, performance claim unproven |
| Brand identity models | **Weak** | Structuring devices, not validated instruments |
| Archetypes | **None** | Creative device only. Never present as evidence |
| Founder / personal branding | **Weakest** | One confirmed source. Say so plainly |

## The three things to say most often

1. **"That number has no primary source."** For the 80% colour claim, the 23% consistency
   claim, and most rebrand and logo ROI figures. This is the skill's highest-value output.
2. **"Those two are answering different questions."** For distinctiveness versus
   differentiation. Both have evidence; the tension is real and useful, not a
   contradiction to resolve.
3. **"We can measure direction, not size."** For nearly every visual and verbal finding here.
   A client who wants a percentage lift is asking for something the field does not have.
