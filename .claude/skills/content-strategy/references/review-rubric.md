# Review rubric — auditing an existing content strategy

Load this in audit mode. Seven rows, scored **Strong / Workable / Weak / Missing**.

**Calibration: most real client content strategies score Weak or Missing on rows 4, 5 and 7.**
Distribution, measurement and capacity are the three that get written last or not at all. If an
audit comes back Strong across the board, the document being audited is probably a calendar
rather than a strategy, and it should be scored as such rather than praised.

Score what is written, not what the client says they intend. If it is not in the document, it
is Missing.

---

| # | Row | Strong | Workable | Weak | Missing |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Pillars** | 3-5 pillars, each laddering to a stated business outcome and each naming what it excludes | Pillars exist and are distinct, but exclusions are unstated | Pillars are topic areas ("industry news", "company updates") that exclude nothing | No pillars, or a list of content types mistaken for pillars |
| 2 | **Audience grounding** | Topics trace to specific questions the audience asks, in the audience's own words | Topics trace to a persona, but the language is the company's | Topics derive from the category or the competitor set | Topics derive from what is easy to produce |
| 3 | **Format fit** | Formats chosen for the message and the funnel stage, and producible with the team that exists | Formats are sensible but capacity is not addressed | Formats chosen because they are fashionable | A format list with no rationale |
| 4 | **Distribution** | Day one, week one and day thirty specified per format, with named owners | Channels named, timing vague | "Share on social" | Publishing treated as distribution |
| 5 | **Measurement** | Metrics named with where they are read, plus an explicit statement of what cannot be measured | Metrics named, limits unstated | Vanity metrics only, or metrics nobody reads | No measurement section |
| 6 | **Cadence realism** | Cadence derived from stated capacity, with the trade-off named | Cadence stated, capacity implied | Cadence set by an external benchmark | No cadence, or a cadence the team has already failed to hold |
| 7 | **Repurposing and evergreen** | An explicit map, plus a refresh trigger for the evergreen set | Repurposing mentioned as a principle | "We'll repurpose" with no mechanism | Neither addressed |

---

## The two gates

Run these before scoring. Either one failing is the headline finding, ahead of any row score.

**The Monday test.** Hand the document to someone who has never met the client and ask what
they would publish on Monday. If they cannot answer from the document alone, the strategy is
not operable, whatever it scores. The usual cause is a missing owner column, not missing ideas.

**The capacity test.** Add up what the plan asks for in hours per week and compare it to the
people who exist. A plan that needs 20 hours a week from a team with 6 is not ambitious, it is
a plan that will be abandoned in month three — and the abandonment costs more than a smaller
plan would have.

---

## What to hand back

**Do not describe everything that is wrong.** An audit returning fourteen findings has not
prioritised, and nothing will be acted on.

1. **The score table**, all seven rows.
2. **The three highest-leverage fixes**, ranked. Rewrite them properly rather than describing
   them: the actual pillar with its exclusion, the actual distribution rows, the actual metric
   table. A rewritten section is worth more than a paragraph about how the section should be
   different.
3. **One thing to stop doing.** Almost every content operation has a format or channel that
   consumes disproportionate effort for nothing. Naming it is usually the most valuable line in
   the audit, and the client will not name it themselves.

If the document turns out to be a calendar or a wish list rather than a strategy, **say that
and offer to run build mode** rather than scoring a document that is not the thing being scored.
