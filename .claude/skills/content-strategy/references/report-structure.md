# Report structure — `18-content-strategy.md`

Section order for the build-mode deliverable. Numbered to sit after `17-conversion-copy.md`
in the client-project series.

Written for the client's team, not for Aleem. Never mentions NexusPoint, Aleem, or this skill.

---

## 0. What we know

A table before any recommendation, so the reader can see what the strategy rests on.

| Fact | Source | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| ... | their brief / their site / their analytics / research / inference | `[verified]` `[research]` `[inferred]` `[assumption]` |

`[verified]` means they told us or we measured it. `[assumption]` means we needed it and did
not have it. **If the upstream documents (`07`, `08`, `13`, `14`) were missing, every fact
derived in their place is `[assumption]` and this table is where that becomes visible.**

---

## 1. Where they are now

The Phase 2 audit, compressed. What they publish, how often, what stopped and when, what
performed, what capacity exists, and what unused raw material is sitting around.

Lead with the finding that will most change their behaviour. If the honest answer is "you
published 40 pieces last year and 3 of them accounted for most of the traffic", that is the
first line of the document.

---

## 2. What this strategy is for

One paragraph. The commercial goal, the audience, and the one thing that has to become true.

If the goal is pipeline, say pipeline. Vague goals produce vague calendars.

---

## 3. Content pillars

Three to five, each as its own block:

**Pillar name** — what it is, in one line.
- **Audience question it answers:** quoted from `08-audience-persona.md` where possible
- **Business outcome:** what it ladders to
- **Role:** educational / thought leadership / promotional / UGC
- **Formats:** which of the 19 suit it, and why
- **Excludes:** what does not belong here even though it is adjacent

Close the section with the **mix** across the four roles, and one sentence on why that mix and
not another. Do not cite the 80/20 or 4-1-1 ratios as evidence for it.

---

## 4. Format and funnel map

One table.

| Pillar | Format | Funnel stage | Channel | Produced by | Effort | Approval |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Effort is a real estimate in hours or days. Approval is the latency, which is usually the
constraint nobody plans around.

---

## 5. Cadence and the 12-week calendar

State the cadence and **why it is that number** — capacity first, then evidence. Say plainly
what was traded away.

Weeks 1-4 specified to the piece:

| Week | Date | Pillar | Format | Channel | Working title | Question it answers | CTA | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Weeks 5-12 at theme level only, one row per week. Note in the document that this is deliberate.

---

## 6. Distribution

Per format, not per piece: **day one**, **week one**, **day thirty**.

Name the channels, the internal amplifiers, the owned audiences, and any partner or syndication
surface. Mark anything that is convention rather than tested as convention.

---

## 7. Repurposing map

The hero asset at the top, derivatives beneath, with the transformation named at each step.

```
Hero: <asset>
 └─ <derivative>   <transformation>            <who/which skill>
```

No multipliers, no "one becomes thirty". Map what this asset can carry.

---

## 8. Evergreen, trending and refresh

The ratio, defended in two sentences. Then the refresh schedule: what gets reviewed, on what
trigger, and the update / consolidate / retire decision. Hand page-level mechanics to
`seo-onpage` by name rather than restating them.

---

## 9. Measurement

| Metric | Where it is read | Who reads it | How often | What it does not tell you |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Then a short honest paragraph on the limits of content attribution in their specific stack, and
what would have to change to measure more. No promised percentages.

---

## 10. What we assumed

Every `[assumption]` from section 0, gathered, each with what would confirm or kill it, and
which of them would most change the plan if wrong.

---

## 11. First 30 days

Six to ten numbered actions, each with an owner and a date. Concrete enough to start Monday:
"publish X", "record Y", "set up Z", not "develop a content culture".

---

## Formatting rules

- No emojis. **No em dashes in body text**; headings may use them.
- Tables over prose wherever the content is structured.
- Every number carries its tier and its source. A `[P]` benchmark is labelled as vendor-
  published; a `[P*]` platform figure carries the date it was retrieved.
- Never name NexusPoint, Aleem, or any internal skill inside the client document. Internal
  routing notes belong in the chat response, not in the file.
